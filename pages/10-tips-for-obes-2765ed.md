# 10 tips for OBEs

This transcript comes from Haley, a practitioner who has consciously practised astral projection and out-of-body experiences (OBEs) for over 13 years, sharing lessons learned through trial and error. The core formula repeated throughout: **deep physical relaxation combined with "a little sliver of awareness"**.

### 1. Body position doesn't matter — comfort does

- The speaker had *most* OBEs on their side, not their back; small discomforts amplify during long meditation and become extremely distracting.
- The aim is "body asleep, mind aware" — so use a position your body can actually fall asleep in.
- **If you fall asleep too easily:** use an unfamiliar position, practise somewhere other than your bed (guest room, couch, a mat beside the bed), or lie reversed in bed with your head where your feet usually go.
- **If you stay awake for hours:** "pretend you're going to sleep" in your most comfortable position, then anchor awareness with vivid imagination, counting down with each breath, daydreaming or an auditory anchor.
- Dipping in and out of sleep is ideal — you can catch the vibrations the moment you surface.

### 2. Wake-back-to-bed (WBTB) makes "mind awake, body asleep" accessible

- Interrupting sleep means the body is already relaxed; you only need to add awareness. It works for lucid dreams and OBEs, but isn't strictly necessary.
- The speaker's reliable routine: wake after **~5 hours** of sleep, stay up a few minutes (journalling, reviewing the technique), then lie down with breath work into deep relaxation.
- **Hypnagogic imagery** — colours, flashes, dream scenes, thoughts that stop making sense, mixing up counted numbers — signals you're near the sleep threshold; keep a sliver of awareness and rehearse your exit technique.
- Waking directly from a dream indicates REM sleep; you can meditate back into it and watch the dream form, or astral project from the lucid dream.

### 3. Deep relaxation is the single biggest skill to build

- The speaker recently attended the **Monroe Institute**, where the core skill practised was entering deep states — "mind awake, body asleep", or **Focus 10** as Robert Monroe calls it — described as the golden, foundational state for expanded awareness.
- Progress went from ~30 minutes to a few minutes to enter the state; in one 15-minute daytime meditation, vibrations began around the 6–7 minute mark.
- In Monroe's book *Journeys Out of the Body*, he had spontaneous OBEs during naps and trained himself to induce the vibrational state within minutes — showing the state is accessible at any time of day.
- Mind awake/body asleep is **not** paralysis: it's simply being so relaxed there's no urge to move, sinking into the surface — reachable faster than people assume.

### 4. Don't wait for the vibrational state — you can induce it

- Vibrations are a natural symptom of sleep paralysis (which occurs several times nightly during REM, roughly every 90 minutes), but they can also arise in ordinary daytime meditation — the speaker has entered them at midday between work sessions, with no prior sleep.
- **Induction visualisation:** imagine a pinpoint of light in the distance drawing closer and expanding — like a star with a powerful magnetic, vibrating pull; feel the tingling climb from your feet and wash up and down the body like a wave. A mental "dial" can turn the intensity up.
- The biggest saboteur is the analytical mind — "Oh my god, it's happening" — which can stop the vibrations entirely. Stay relaxed, let them stabilise, then use your exit technique.

### 5. Not everybody experiences a separation phase

- Separation can feel like slipping out, slowly spinning, falling out of bed, floating up or sinking beneath the bed — but it may not occur at all.
- **Monroe Institute anecdote:** in a dark, sensory-deprived "check unit" cubby, the speaker felt an astral arm in an odd position, imagined rolling out — and their vision suddenly cleared, already out of body beside the window bench, with the sunlight later matching exactly what was "seen". No separation sensations at all.
- Fixating on *feeling* separation can delay the experience; *imagining yourself out* can place your awareness there directly and skip the phase entirely.
- **If you visualise poorly, use memory:** physically walk out of your room beforehand, noting textures and details, then re-experience that walk from memory during the technique — like recalling a favourite memory through non-visual senses.

### 6. Plan your exit technique, focus mantra and OBE plan beforehand

- **Choose ONE exit technique** (roll-out, rope, sinking beneath the bed, target technique) and stick with it — cycling through techniques mid-vibrations disperses your energy and can end the experience.
- Multi-sensory engagement is key to exiting: rehearse sitting up, walking around, touching walls, exploring rooms in vivid detail.
- **Focus mantra** — one phrase repeated throughout to keep the mind clear and awake, e.g. *"Now I separate from my body"*, *"Now I am out of body"*, *"Out of body now"*.
- **OBE plan** — decide in advance what you'll do once out, so spontaneous experiences don't collapse in panic.
- Keep an **astral projection journal** tracking techniques, times, results and your plan; refine until it succeeds, then swap in a new one.

### 7. Try your technique at all times of day and night

- The speaker has succeeded during the day, during WBTB and early morning — don't fixate on one window.
- Repeating the same wake-up time backfires: the brain anticipates it and wakes you too alert. Their nightly 3:30–4 a.m. WBTB stopped working because they began waking *before* the alarm, already too awake.
- Don't practise every single day — allow rest and recharge, and vary timings to strengthen the underlying skill.

### 8. Set your intention to have an OBE — then release it

- Nothing blocks an OBE more than forcing it: over-effort keeps the mind too awake and suppresses the very sensations you're waiting for.
- Redirect focus to **skill development in each phase** — how long you can hold mind awake/body asleep, how much deeper you can relax than last time. Comfort and enjoyment help you go deeper, faster.
- Give some sessions narrower goals (just the vibrations, just depth, just maintaining the state) rather than expecting an OBE every time.
- Frustration and irritation create expectation and stall progress; cultivate fun, curiosity and gratitude — removing resistance makes the experience more likely.

### 9. Develop your dream work and lucid dreaming skills

- In a lucid dream you're already in REM, with the body paralysed and vibrating while conscious — an ideal launchpad for OBEs.
- **Routes from lucid dream to OBE:** will yourself awake into the vibrations; affirm you're out of body; or (the speaker's favourite) **fly straight upward as fast as possible** — this dissolves the dream and can eject you out of body, sometimes with a spinning separation sensation, bypassing the difficult separation phase entirely.
- **"Dual body awareness"** — feeling your physical and dream body (or dream and astral body) simultaneously — can occur.
- Habitual daytime reality checks ("could I be dreaming?") carry into dreams, producing spontaneous lucidity you can use for OBEs without mid-night wake-ups.

### 10. Rehearse the vibrations and separation phase

- Purpose: reduce fear, surprise and over-excitement, and teach the brain what separation feels like so it seems natural when it happens.
- The speaker claims *"your brain cannot tell the difference between something you're imagining and something that's actually happening"* — so rehearsing calm, alert responses trains the real event.
- Practical method: before bed, imagine separating, walking around your home and staying aware; if you've felt vibrations once, replay that memory as if it's happening again.
- **What vibrations feel like:** buzzy and tingling all over — like hitting your funny bone, but amplified across the whole body — sometimes with auditory sounds and colours or flashes under closed eyes. Over-excitement ends the experience, so rehearsed calm is protective.
- Overall advice: take the pressure off, journal your attempts, and build a personal "map or recipe" of what works for you.
