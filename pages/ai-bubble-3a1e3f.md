## Significant points

1. **The $3.2 trillion figure is genuine and correctly attributed.** KBRA's August 2026 report puts gross disclosed contractual commitments plus maximum contingent support across the "AI-7" at roughly $3.2tn, up from $575bn at end-2024 — a 5.6× increase in about eighteen months [1].
2. **The AI-7 list matches KBRA exactly**: Meta, Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft, Nvidia, Broadcom, Oracle [1:1].
3. **Three off-balance-sheet buckets** (uncommenced leases, unconditional purchase obligations, guarantees/backstops) is an accurate description of the mechanics.
4. **Alphabet's quarter-on-quarter jump is real**: purchase commitments and other contractual obligations of $332bn at 31 March 2026 rising to $811bn at 30 June 2026 [2][3].
5. **Meta's Hyperion financing is real and the numbers check out**: $27.3bn of 6.581% senior secured notes priced 16 October 2025, due May 2049, rated A+, issued via Beignet Investor LLC, the largest US corporate bond on record, backed by a Meta parent guarantee and a residual-value guarantee [4][5][6][7].
6. **Oracle's uncommenced lease pile is real**: $288bn, mostly 15–19-year data-centre leases, up from ~$260bn at 31 May 2026 [8].
7. **Meta's negative free cash flow is real and correctly dated**: Q3 2026 cash from operations $32.79bn against capex including finance-lease principal of $34.91bn, giving free cash flow of –$2.12bn, the first negative quarter since 2012 [9][10].
8. **CoreWeave's distress is real**: its 2031 and 2032 unsecured notes traded below 80 cents in mid-October 2026 with yields above 15% [11][12][13].
9. **Anthropic's compute commitments are real** : the public S-1 (26 October 2026) dsclosed $542–567bn [14]; the amended prospectus filed 2 November 2026 put aggregate committed spend at about $895.4bn [15].
10. **Circular financing (suppliers financing customers)** is the strongest analytical point, and the Broadcom/Anthropic link is real: Broadcom is Anthropic's largest infrastructure partner in 2026, with $42bn of "potential financing" [14:1].
11. **Balance point is legitimate**: KBRA itself notes six of the AI-7 hold lease-adjusted leverage below 1.5× and that commitments are "not economically uniform" — leases, capacity commitments and contingent support should not be treated as one debt-like block [1:2]. The video's headline comparison of a multi-year stock of commitments against one year of revenue is therefore rhetorically useful but not like-for-like.
