# Amulet of Samarkand

![Amulet of Samarkand](https://storage.ghost.io/c/9c/2a/9c2aa86d-156e-419f-9a0f-38bf91aebf15/content/images/size/w2000/2026/11/Amulet.jpg)

In an alternate London where magicians rule the British Empire and commoners are governed by them, Nathaniel — a purchased, legally nameless child — is apprenticed at five to Arthur Underwood, a mediocre Ministry magician who resents him. At ten, Nathaniel is paraded before a gathering of magicians and publicly humiliated by the charismatic, ambitious Simon Lovelace, while Underwood stands by and does nothing. Embittered, he teaches himself magic in secret and, through a scrying glass, watches Lovelace take delivery of the Amulet of Samarkand — an artifact he intends to use to seize the government.

Days after his twelfth birthday, Nathaniel summons the djinni Bartimaeus and orders him to steal the Amulet, then hides it in his own master's study to frame him. Bartimaeus is caught spying and imprisoned in the Tower of London, where he's interrogated by the security minister Jessica Whitwell; he escapes, but unwittingly leads Lovelace back to the Underwood house. Lovelace finds the Amulet in the study, Nathaniel defiantly reveals himself as the thief, and Underwood — desperate — urges Lovelace to kill the boy instead of him. Lovelace's spirits destroy the house and the Underwoods die; Bartimaeus saves Nathaniel. That cowardice is the novel's real betrayal, and it makes Nathaniel an orphan twice over.

The two travel to Heddleham Hall to stop Lovelace's coup. Bartimaeus fights a bearded mercenary who is eerily resistant to magic; Nathaniel is caught and given an ultimatum by Lovelace's mentor Schyler — join them or die — and kills Schyler with firework-cubes. At the conference Nathaniel tries to warn the magicians and is sealed in a bubble by Whitwell while Lovelace, shielded by the Amulet, blows an ancient horn and summons **Ramuthra**, a spirit of annihilating scale that kills magicians and spirits indiscriminately.

Bartimaeus distracts Lovelace; Nathaniel snatches the Amulet, Ramuthra obliterates Lovelace, and Nathaniel speaks the words of dismissal and breaks the horn. He hands the Amulet to Prime Minister Devereaux and becomes the government's quiet hero. In return for Bartimaeus keeping his true name secret, Nathaniel promises never to summon him again — and unbinds him, earning the djinni's grudging respect as he begins a true magician's life under Whitwell.

Note: the Kitty/Resistance thread is present but a subplot here; it becomes central in book two.

---

**Full spoiler warning.** All 44 chapters, in nine grouped blocks. The book runs in three parts (1–14, 15–30, 31–44) and alternates narrators, with each chapter headed either "Bartimaeus" (first person, footnotes) or "Nathaniel" (third person).

---

### Ch. 1–4 — "Bartimaeus": The heist

A twelve-year-old boy summons Bartimaeus into an attic pentacle and orders him to steal the **Amulet of Samarkand** from **Simon Lovelace**. Bartimaeus flies to Hampstead, scouts the villa on all seven planes, tunnels under the wards as a mole, then infiltrates a party as a fly and shifts to human form to crack the strongroom. He takes the Amulet, sets off every alarm, scraps briefly with a spirit in the kitchen (**Faquarl**), and escapes as a peregrine falcon.

### Ch. 5, 8, 9 — "Nathaniel": The making of the boy

Flashbacks, out of order. At six he's sent to fetch spectacles and is mobbed by imps invisible without them — mute for a week. At five, **Arthur Underwood**, a middling Ministry magician, is ordered to take an apprentice; the boy's records are wiped and he becomes nameless, while Arthur's wife **Martha** breaks the rules to give him warmth and her own name. Then the long grind of tutoring: languages, history, the doctrine that demons are wicked, and the first lessons in seeing through illusion.

### Ch. 6–7, 10–11 — "Bartimaeus": Hiding, and the frame

Dodging Lovelace's search spheres, Bartimaeus hides in a sewage works and then Trafalgar Square, where a gang of children — led by a bold girl — try to take the Amulet off him. At dawn he's summoned back and finds **Adelbrand's Pentacle**, which extends his servitude. The boy doesn't want the Amulet for himself: he orders Bartimaeus to hide it in Underwood's own study, framing his master. Bartimaeus does it as a spider, and overhears that Nathaniel's first summoning is coming.

### Ch. 12–14 — "Nathaniel" then "Bartimaeus": The wound, and the tin

The key flashback: a garden summer with his tutor **Ms. Lutyens**, then a party where **Lovelace** humiliates Nathaniel with magic while Underwood does nothing, and Lutyens is dismissed for taking his side. Nathaniel teaches himself in secret, summons an imp with no name, learns Lovelace has the Amulet and that a man died for it, and plots for six months. Then the duel: Bartimaeus wears **Ptolemy's** face to needle him, counters every spell by speaking Nathaniel's birth name, and is finally beaten by a **tobacco tin** and the Spell of Indefinite Confinement.

### Ch. 15–17 — Poisoned victory

Nathaniel binds the tin, panics that a djinni knows his name, is named **John Mandrake**, and throws the tin into the Thames. Bartimaeus, meanwhile, intercepts a messenger imp and reads Lovelace's letters to the **Prime Minister Devereaux** and to **Schyler**. At **Pinn's Accoutrements** he learns the Amulet was stolen from a murdered government magician, **Beecham**, and holds something powerful — then is caught in a silver net.

### Ch. 18–20 — Parliament

Nathaniel, ill and nervous, is taken to the state address at Westminster Hall, where Devereaux boasts of military strength. A wild-eyed boy on the terrace throws a sphere that shatters into **elementals**; magicians are hurt, and Nathaniel gives the only coherent description of the attacker while the adults blame the **Resistance**.

### Ch. 21–25 — The Tower

Bartimaeus wakes inside a **Mournful Orb** in the Tower of London, interrogated by **Jessica Whitwell** about his master and the Amulet, and is nearly killed by the utukku **Baztuk** before two ravens — **Faquarl** and **Jabor** — slaughter the guards. Faquarl melts an iron ring so Bartimaeus can squeeze out as a fly; they flee across London, and when Faquarl presses him for the Amulet's location, Bartimaeus sets him alight and runs.

### Ch. 26–30 — The house burns

Nathaniel's failed summons, Underwood catching him, the imp revealing Bartimaeus is in the Tower. Lovelace arrives at the house; Nathaniel confesses to the theft to protect the Underwoods, and Lovelace — declaring no magician can ever be honourable — orders them killed. **Arthur dies**; **Jabor** destroys the house; Bartimaeus drags Nathaniel to the roof, collapses it under Jabor, and they escape into the night. **Martha is dead**.

### Ch. 31–34 — Vagrancy

Nathaniel, freezing and guilty, bargains: Bartimaeus helps him get justice against Lovelace, and is freed at the end of it — with licence to kill Nathaniel if he's reckless. They learn the fire is blamed on Nathaniel, and that Lovelace means to strike at a conference at **Heddleham Hall**. Nathaniel loses his scrying glass to commoner children, and a girl named **Kitty** decides not to kill him.

### Ch. 35–37 — Infiltration

They ambush a grocer's van, take the driver's and son's identities, and bluff past a **bearded mercenary** at the gate — Bartimaeus reciting an absurd grocery list from memory. Nathaniel knocks out a pageboy with a rolling pin and takes his uniform, then serves canapés among the magicians as Devereaux arrives.

### Ch. 38–40 — Schyler, and the trap

Lovelace and **Schyler** corner Nathaniel in the library: Lovelace will kill the ministers with a summoned **afrit**, and Schyler offers Nathaniel a place in the new regime. He refuses and insults them. Bartimaeus fights the mercenary and loses. Nathaniel brings Schyler down with a barrage of **Prague Cubes** — party tricks full of tiny imps — and reaches the hall too late.

### Ch. 41–42 — Ramuthra

Lovelace blows the **summoning horn**; the hall becomes a **Stricture** trapping Nathaniel and Bartimaeus inside a giant pentacle. Something vast tears a rift: **Ramuthra**, which kills indiscriminately. Nathaniel leaps at Lovelace, tears the Amulet off him, and Lovelace is destroyed; then Nathaniel speaks the dismissal and breaks the horn, and the rift closes.

### Ch. 43–44 — The story he tells

Nathaniel presents the Amulet to **Devereaux** and gives a performance of heroism in which Bartimaeus is a "minor imp"; the ministers swallow it whole. Under **Whitwell** as his new master, he boasts of hunting the Resistance. Then he keeps his word and dismisses Bartimaeus, who leaves in a stink of brimstone — free, and quietly impressed.
