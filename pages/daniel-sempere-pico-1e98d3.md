## Key Points on AI

### The shift from prompting to agents

- The biggest change has been **agentic AI**, which he attributes largely to "OpenClaw" (Claude Code), which became the fastest-growing open-source software around the beginning of the year.
- Prompt engineering is largely obsolete: "the less you tell the AI or the more room you give it to pick the best path… the better it is" — describe the *outcome*, not the method.
- Trying to keep up with every release is futile; skills, commands, and `CLAUDE.md` files will all eventually be "abstracted away." AI is "the first software where you don't even have to know how to use it — you can just ask it to tell you how you should use it."

### His personal setup

- **OpenClaw (via Telegram)**: mainly a health and fitness bot — logs his food, workouts, and personal records (e.g. bench press progress since December).
- **Codex desktop app** (OpenAI): his primary tool for copywriting (trained on clients' blogs and posts), admin tasks, and learning. Example: drafting an insurance email and having the agent find and attach the required documents itself.
- **Perplexity** for search ("a better Google"); **NotebookLM** occasionally; a **Plaud** device on the back of his phone for voice-noting ideas (having previously built the Limitless Pen, later bought by Meta and shut down).
- He values that his messy file system no longer matters — the AI can find anything.

### Model preferences

- **Writing/editing**: Gemini produces the most natural text; Anthropic's Claude models close behind; GPT/Codex models weakest. Gemini repeatedly produced fresh sentence variations after other models ran out.
- **Agentic tasks**: OpenAI models good enough; Claude Code beat Gemini CLI on a Gmail task.
- **Front-end/design**: Opus is best; Codex is "not a great designer."
- **Image AI**: useful for prototyping and website design, but not final graphics. Many people already fall for AI-generated video (older family friends sharing fake videos as real).

### Cost management

- Subscribes to ChatGPT Pro (~$100), Claude Max (~$100), and Gemini via Google Workspace (~$20) — roughly **$200/month**, never hitting limits because he uses each for different purposes.
- ChatGPT Pro can be used on third-party tools, providing "heavily subsidised" tokens; Anthropic doesn't allow this. He'd pay $1,000/month if needed, comparing the cost to hiring an assistant.
- Previously managed on the $20 tiers until January this year.

### Privacy, security and sovereignty

- He abandoned de-Googling, Linux and GrapheneOS — the time cost outweighed the benefit, and he'd "rather just have the convenience."
- Notes the privacy/convenience trade-off is direct: consumer Gmail/Drive and WhatsApp data are likely used for training (Google Workspace accounts are the exception).
- His main motivation for **local AI** isn't privacy but access — not wanting intelligence "gatekept" by companies or governments, likening it to being banned from Google or social media.
- Plans to buy the most powerful Mac Studio when new ones ship and run local models. Acknowledges the RAM/GPU price surge, estimating ~$10,000–15,000 for a 1 TB Mac Studio — worth it only for tinkerers or the self-sovereignty-minded. Most people are well served by smaller models (e.g. Google's Gemma 4) on reasonable hardware, and he expects phone-class Opus-level models within a decade.

### Maintaining voice in writing

- The only tool that truly replicates his voice is a custom OpenClaw skill ("Postie App") trained on ~63,000 of his tweets, used for short-form content.
- Otherwise, he writes first and uses AI strictly as an **editor**, intervening on the smallest possible units — sentence by sentence, or asking for alternative words, logic gaps, and flow issues.
- Argues AI can't fully ghost-write for you because you often don't know what the finished piece looks like until writing reveals it — asking AI to write like you would be "asking it to predict the future."

### Advice for beginners

- Forget tactics, skills, and prompt engineering: "Using AI is essentially just the skill of being able to describe an outcome that you would like to happen."
- Speak to it as you would to another human, and ask of every daily task: "How can I do this with AI?"
- Credits this tip to **Dharmesh Shah, co-founder of HubSpot**: try to use AI every time you need to do anything on a computer.
- He now does AI consulting for corporate employees whose companies restrict AI use.
