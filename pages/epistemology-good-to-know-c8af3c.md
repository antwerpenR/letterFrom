### 1. The central thesis

Humans never interact with reality directly; they operate on **abstract linguistic maps** of reality. Language compresses a rich, multi-dimensional experience ("deep structure") into a narrow, linear string of words ("surface structure"). This compression is governed by three structural rules that can be **weaponised** to construct false realities, but because they are grammatical they leave **structural artefacts** that can be algorithmically recovered.

### 2. The "metamodel of epistemological recovery"

Duke presents the NLP metamodel as the toolkit for auditing how language has been manipulated. He frames the explainer video (lines 75–170) as the formal statement of the framework.

### 3. A "missing" curriculum

Duke argues the classical **trivium and quadrivium** are incomplete. He proposes adding three disciplines he believes get a person closer to reality than the traditional seven:

- **Linguistics**
- **Epistemology** ("how do you know that?")
- **Ontology**

He claims the **10 metamodel patterns** (deletion, distortion, generalisation) cover the ~3,000 logical and scientific fallacies catalogued on "the Ethical Skeptic" website, making them simpler and faster to learn.

### 4. The "map is not the territory"

Attributed to **Alfred Korzybski**, taught to **Stuart Chase**. Unconscious people mistake their map *for* the territory. Duke links this to the Greek word **syniadesis**, which he treats as the Greek form of "consciousness" — the ability to recognise that you *have* a frame.

### 5. The "logos" nominalisation — Duke's origin story

The nominalisation that "really got me started" is the translation of the Greek **logos** in John 1:1. He argues **logos** is an *active process* (conveying ideas through language) but was frozen into the static noun "the word" — a political translation decision he traces to the **Council of Nicaea**.

### 6. The Chomsky "misdirection" thesis (speculative)

Duke advances a self-described "gut feeling": that *Manufacturing Consent* was promoted to make Chomsky famous for something other than **Syntactic Structures** (1957), the foundational book for NLP, so that people doing background checks wouldn't examine it. He explicitly flags this as speculation — "a finger in the air… I don't have substantive proof on this."

### 7. Cause-effect and complex equivalence — the two patterns

Duke credits **Matteo Morelli** for telling him he only needs to learn two patterns, used together:

- **Cause-effect** — "A causes B" / "this happened *because* that happened"
- **Complex equivalence** — "this *means* that"

He says politicians rarely speak without saying "A caused B because C."

### 8. Robert Dilts's "Sleight of Mouth" — 14 reframes

Dilts, a student/associate of Grinder and Bandler, identified **14 ways** to dismantle a cause-effect / complex-equivalence statement. Duke gives four worked examples against the statement *"protesting against genocide in Gaza means someone is anti-Semitic"*:

1. **Challenge the implied causation** — how does a position on a military action produce a position on an entire ethnicity?
2. **Counter-example** — has anyone protested a military action and remained sympathetic to the associated people?
3. **Chunking up** — does opposing any government's military policy make a person hostile to the associated ethnic group? (step out of the false dichotomy and reframe)
4. **Apply to the self** — does supporting military operations in Gaza make someone anti-Palestinian? (complete inversion)

### 9. The "render unto Caesar" example — metamodel in the New Testament

Duke's self-described "most important point": the red-letter New Testament conversations of Jesus are **interrogative in nature** and specifically target deletion, distortion and generalisation. The Pharisees + Herodians set a **double bind** (answer "yes" → lose the Jewish base; answer "no" → reported as a seditionist). Jesus reframes by asking a question — a "metaframe / sleight of mouth" example he says is ~2,000 years old. His conclusion: **Dilts, Grinder, Bandler, Chomsky, Korzybski and Chase did not invent these techniques; they documented them.**

## Key lists extracted verbatim from the transcript

### The three linguistic compression mechanisms (from the explainer video)

1. **Deletion** — crucial relational information left out entirely (e.g. "they made a mistake" → ask *who specifically? what specifically?*)
2. **Distortion** — presenting subjective interpretation as objective fact; most significant form is **nominalisation** (freezing a dynamic process into a static noun: *inform* → *misinformation*; *inquiring* → *the science*)
3. **Generalisation** — stretching a limited set of variables to a universal scope (e.g. "people **always** let me down")

### The metamodel recovery interrogatives

**how, what, who, when, where, why**

### The three added disciplines Duke proposes for the trivium/quadrivium

1. Linguistics
2. Epistemology
3. Ontology

### The intellectual lineage Duke traces (chronological)

**Korzybski → Bateson → Chomsky → Lifton → Bandler → Dilts → present day**

### The four Sleight of Mouth examples given (against "protesting genocide in Gaza means someone is anti-Semitic")

1. Challenge the implied causation
2. Counter-example
3. Chunking up (step out of the frame and reframe)
4. Apply to the self (inversion)

### Lifton's two at-scale techniques

1. Loaded language (e.g. "bourgeois mentality")
2. Thought-terminating clichés (e.g. "trust the science," "it is what it is")

## Accuracy check (to guard against hallucination)

A few of Duke's attributions check out cleanly against the sources; a couple are contested or overstated. Flagging these so the review doesn't launder his claims:

- ✅ **Stuart Chase, *The Tyranny of Words* (1938), drawing on Korzybski** — confirmed. The 1938 Harcourt, Brace edition exists, and reviewers explicitly note Chase "borrows" from Korzybski's general semantics, including levels of abstraction [1][2].
- ✅ **Robert Dilts, Sleight of Mouth, 14 patterns, targeting cause-effect and complex equivalence** — confirmed. Multiple NLP sources state Dilts modelled Bandler's argument skills and produced 14 original patterns that specifically disrupt complex-equivalence and cause-effect structures [3][4].
- ✅ **Gregory Bateson, double bind theory, schismogenesis** — confirmed. Bateson and colleagues first stated double-bind theory in the 1950s (the 1956 paper "Toward a Theory of Schizophrenia"); Bateson also coined *schismogenesis* [5][6].
