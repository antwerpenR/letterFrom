### **The Evolution: From Flirt to Obsession**

The journey began pragmatically: a **$25 iMac from Marketplace** intended for reading and writing. After a successful macOS upgrade via OpenCore Patcher, viewer suggestions prompted a Linux Mint installation. The initial experience defied expectations—installation took 30 minutes, not the anticipated hours.

Within two weeks, the relationship "went from a flirt to something more serious." Foci replaced "nightly doom scrolling" with browsing Marketplace, acquiring a **free 2010 netbook** for MX Linux experimentation. When Windows 10 support ended, he migrated his 2017 gaming PC—though Nvidia GTX 1070 drivers proved "rough."

The hardware acquisitions escalated: an iMac Pro, M1 Air, and a **free ThinkPad** that became his 90% daily driver. Ironically, this 8GB DDR4/eighth-gen i5 machine outperforms expectations for university work, writing, and video development in 2026. He currently runs **CachyOS with KDE Plasma**, appreciating its lack of an app store because "that really motivates me to learn the terminal."

---

### **Significant and Surprising Points**

**Surprising Hardware Longevity:**

- A 2010 netbook and 2017 gaming PC received extended life through Linux
- The free ThinkPad (8GB RAM, 8th-gen i5) serves as primary 2026 workstation

**Unexpected Psychological Effects:**

- Using old hardware "just felt great"
- Learning terminal commands made him "feel very cool"
- Replaced "doom scrolling" with hardware hunting

**The "Paralysis by Analysis" Phenomenon:**

- Every Linux project "opens three new doors"
- Simple goals (host music library) cascade into storage, containers, backups, remote access
- Found himself "watching 45-minute videos on Docker at midnight" despite only conceiving the project two days prior

**The Streaming Service Critique:**  
Foci offers a philosophical rejection of algorithmic media consumption:

- Playlists create "fragmented experience" like "taking your favorite episodes from different TV shows"
- Album listening provides "the full story, the artist's intentional curation"
- Compares Netflix scrolling to song browsing: "even though I'm just supposed to take out the trash. That's stupid"

**Community Gatekeeping:**

- Received comments demanding: "Do you actually read the code? If not, why are you using open-source software?"
- His response: "No, I absolutely cannot read code. I just find the open-source philosophy appealing"
- Suspects gatekeepers don't read all code themselves

### **Home Lab Roadmap**

Foci's planned progression:

1. **NAS construction** (immediate next project)
2. **Media server** with physical media digitization
3. **Pi-hole for ad blocking**

His motivations include digital independence from "Google, Apple, and Microsoft" and running self-hosted alternatives: cloud storage, backups, VPNs, and media servers.

---

### **Digital Privacy Descent**

The privacy rabbit hole began with incremental changes:

- Open-source/privacy-oriented browser (replacing Chrome)
- Alternative search engine
- Ad blocker

This expanded to investigating private email, VPNs, encrypted messaging, and custom DNS—culminating in consideration of **Graphene OS phone replacement** for his iPhone.

---

### **Persistent Challenges**

**Technical Friction Points:**

- OBS screen recording conflicts with 4K monitor
- Recurring microphone and "small box" issues
- Accumulation of "small annoyances" that don't drive abandonment but create friction

**The Editing Dilemma (Primary Obstacle):**

- Final Cut Pro on iMac works "exceptionally well"
- Kdenlive experiments unsatisfying: "I just don't really like it"
- M4 Mac Mini ($600) offers better value than Linux PC build for 4K editing
- Missing gaming motivates potential future Linux PC build

---

### **Conclusion**

Foci describes his position as "very much in a position of paralysis by analysis" yet emphasizes the rabbit hole is "one that I like to be in." The six-month arc demonstrates how Linux adoption frequently transcends operating system preference—becoming gateway to broader digital autonomy movements. His non-technical perspective validates accessibility while honestly documenting the learning curve's psychological and practical dimensions.
