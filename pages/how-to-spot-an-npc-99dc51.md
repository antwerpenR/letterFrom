## The Therapist Anecdote

Robinson frames the episode around his frustration with slow, oblivious people encountered daily — at airports, grocery stores, on roads. He recounts that in the late 1990s or early 2000s, a girlfriend suggested he had an anger problem. He saw a therapist once. Her conclusion: **"You are of high intelligence and you live in a world of very mid to low intelligence… You're in a sea of idiots."** She told him: **"You're not crazy. You just have a low tolerance for stupidity."**

## The NPC Concept

Robinson defines NPCs by analogy to video game non-player characters — people on "predetermined routes," walking the same patterns daily, seemingly without independent thought. He entertains **simulation theory**, suggesting that if we live in a simulation, NPCs make sense as entities existing to populate the world without consuming excessive computing power. He references DMT experiences ("seeing the grid") as supporting this possibility. He cites cultural touchstones: **Free Guy** (Ryan Reynolds as an NPC), **The Truman Show**, and **The Matrix** (the red dress scene). He draws an analogy to video game rendering — NPCs may not "exist" until they enter your field of interaction, likening this to Schrödinger's thought experiment.

## Historical Parallels

Robinson connects the NPC concept to research for his forthcoming book on eugenics. He describes how **British colonial administrators** in Rhodesia, South Africa, and India dealt with populations they considered intellectually inferior. Colonial policy, he says, was to **not fraternise** with natives, to **command rather than reason**, and to use force because reasoning was deemed futile. He references the **British Round Table** and suggests these administrators were, in effect, dealing with NPCs. He also references **Aztec human sacrifice** as an example of how historical societies dealt with expendable populations. He explicitly states he is "not going to break out an actual whip."

## NPC Characteristics

Robinson lists observable traits:

- **Dullness / soullessness** — a visible "dullness" in the eyes; operating at a base level of awareness
- **No situational awareness** — inability to assess safety or surroundings (he cites "60 people a day killed by trains in India")
- **Shallow interactions** — limited conversation, limited understanding of current events
- **"SSR eyes"** — appearing chemically subdued by SSRIs
- **"Aimless, medicated, vaccinated, castrated"** — his characterisation of the NPC demographic
- **Looping** — repeating one phrase under stress (e.g., arrest videos: "Why am I being arrested?" on repeat)
- **Obesity** — he states categorically: "super fat people are NPCs," citing *My 600-lb Life* and motorised scooter users at Disney
- **No planning ability** — they react to life rather than plan for future events
- **Need for authority** — attracted to hierarchical/pyramidal structures; comfortable at the bottom
- **No critical thinking** — cannot problem-solve, analyse, or think in words; think in images at best

### The "Breakfast Test"

The diagnostic question: **"Imagine how you would feel if you didn't have breakfast today."** The expected answer: **"I'd probably be hungry."** The NPC answer: **"But I did have breakfast today"** — an inability to engage in counterfactual reasoning. Robinson says this question is used "to determine whether or not you're dealing with somebody of limited IQ."

## Psychology Experiments

### Elevator Test (Conformity)

People enter an elevator where everyone faces an unusual direction; the majority conform and face the same way.

### Hypnosis and Suggestibility

Some people are highly susceptible to hypnotic suggestion. He references **Benny Hinn** (faith healer): "If you go to church and you're dancing with serpents and the preacher… takes the devil out of you, you're a [expletive] NPC."

### Stanford Prison Experiment — Dr. Philip Zimbardo

Guards and prisoners randomly assigned; **shut down after 6 days** (planned for 2 weeks) because guards "turned psychopathic instantly." Funded by the **US Office of Naval Research**. 24 participants. Takeaway: "All some people need is permission to be a monster and they will be a monster."

### Milgram Experiment — Obedience to Authority

White lab coat + clipboard; participants told to administer increasingly powerful electric shocks. Robinson states approximately **70% of people continued to crank up the shock** when instructed by a perceived authority figure. He references **Peter Hotez** wearing a lab coat during COVID interviews as a "prop" and "psychological tactic." Takeaway: "Just following orders was a perfect excuse for NPCs."

### Asch Conformity Experiment — Line Length Test

A subject is shown a card with three lines and asked which is longest. Four confederates give the wrong answer.

- **~35% of responses conformed** to the group's wrong answer (approximately one in three)
- Only **26% of participants consistently defied** the majority opinion

He calls this **"distortion of perception"** — people who fear *being different* more than they fear *being wrong*. He extrapolates: "We're not talking about will you or won't you take the vaccines. Imagine what that does to the numbers."

NPCs are attracted to hierarchical/pyramidal structures where they can occupy the bottom rung.

## How to Manage NPCs

- **Limit options**: Don't give 30 tasks at once; give 2 tasks, then 2 more — too many options causes shutdown
- **Command, don't reason**: Instruct NPCs directly; reasoning does not work
- He compares this to business management courses: "they can't come right out and say the world is a human farm and you're going to have to manage the sheep"

## The "Haunted House" Metaphor (credited to Sam Tripoli)

NPCs go through life like people shuffling through a haunted house — reacting to stimuli, fear, and surprise, unable to go faster or slower, just responding to whatever jumps out at them. He draws a parallel to how fear is inserted into society to provoke reactions.

6. **Accept that some people can't be fixed** — "Some people just aren't going to make it. You're going to have to let them go."
7. **Avoid NPCs where possible** — "They are time wasters. They are obstacles to be avoided."
