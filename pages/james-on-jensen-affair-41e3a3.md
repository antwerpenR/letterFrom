### 1. The framing: it isn't about the affair

Corbett's central claim is that the sex scandal is the "infotainment bait", and the real question is **why this story is being reported now**, given that comparable skeletons presumably exist in other tech CEOs' closets and go unreported. His answer: it is about "the staged, engineered, manipulated World War III conflict that is coming into view" — specifically, disrupting US–China rapprochement on the eve of the APEC summit.

He is explicit that this is **not a defence of Huang**: he accepts Huang probably had multiple affairs and that "you pay the price if you play the game". The question he pursues is *who* is calling him out and *for what reason*.

### 2. What Nvidia is (the explainer segment)

Corbett concedes the simple description — Nvidia makes the GPUs fuelling the generative AI boom — but cites an explainer noting the company also designs CPUs, networking, complete servers and racks, cloud services, developer tools, enterprise software, simulation platforms and embedded computers.

### 3. Timeline of the scandal (as given in the episode)

Drawn from a CBS News/Yahoo Finance timeline, a Bloomberg report and other wire copy:

| Date | Event |
| --- | --- |
| Nov 2015 | Alice Huang (no relation), a former Chinese television reporter, joins Shanghai Hexi Investment Management as deputy general manager |
| Mar 2023 | "Seven Road International" renamed Megaspeed; ownership of the "neo cloud" company transferred to Huang Li Limited, controlled by Alice Huang |
| Oct 2023 | Nvidia designates Shanghai Century Lightong as a preferred cloud partner, giving it priority access to Nvidia chips |
| Feb 2025 | Singapore opens investigation into Megaspeed and others over suspected false declarations on server purchases |
| Jul 2025 | Jensen Huang says Nvidia won Trump administration approval to sell advanced H200 (transcript also says "H-20") AI chips to China, reversing prior policy |
| Feb 2026 | Singapore bars Alice Huang from re-entry after she downplayed her role at Megaspeed |
| May 2026 | Secretary of State Marco Rubio briefs Trump on the counterintelligence risk of Huang's affair with Alice Huang |

| That week | Reuters reports a second affair, with Nvidia subordinate Kimberly Powell; a third affair is then reported |
| Next day | Huang resigns |

Bloomberg's reporting is cited for the claim that a firm founded by Alice Huang produced **smuggled AI chips installed at a Shanghai data centre linked to Megaspeed** — described as the most explicit evidence yet of a network circumventing US trade restrictions.

### 4. The "why now": APEC

Mint (Live Mint) is cited: the APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting was scheduled for **Shenzhen, 18–19 November 2026**, with Trump expected to meet Xi Jinping on the sidelines. The article itself judged the row would not "fundamentally disrupt" US–China relations, but would increase domestic pressure on Trump, with lawmakers of both parties demanding scrutiny of Nvidia's China dealings and of semiconductor export policy.

### 5. The precedent: the 2023 "Chinese spy balloon"

Corbett replays contemporaneous news audio from February 2023 (balloon tracked over Montana near a nuclear missile facility; shot down off the Carolinas) to establish a pattern:

- **Effect:** Reuters reported Blinken postponed a long-awaited China trip — a blow to efforts to stabilise the relationship.
- **Official downplaying:** the Pentagon said the balloon posed no military or physical threat and one official assessed it had "limited additive value" for intelligence collection.
- **Later payload:** two years on, a mostly classified US intelligence report (reported by *Newsweek* and others) said the balloon carried a satellite communications module, sensors and tech **from at least five American firms** — underlining the failure of export controls and raising questions about firms such as Nvidia selling dual-use technology globally.

The pattern he identifies: a summit or diplomatic opening is derailed by a hyped scandal whose punchline is "you've been trading with the enemy".

### 6. The detail that undercuts the official story

Corbett flags a Reuters report published **days before** the scandal broke: *"Nvidia chips pile up in Hong Kong as China withholds import approval"* — advanced AI chips (H200s) stockpiled in Hong Kong awaiting shipment because **Beijing was withholding approval** for large-scale imports. His point: if Beijing had run a long-game spy operation to obtain these chips, why was it simultaneously refusing to let them in?

### 6b. Three competing explanations he floats (explicitly not mutually exclusive)

1. **The AI open-vs-closed fight.** Nvidia is among the few AI players with an interest in open models (anyone can run AI locally; you only need the chips), whereas OpenAI, Anthropic and others lobby for regulation that keeps out open competition. Ousting Huang removes an open-model advocate.
2. **The Israel angle.** Interim CEO **Colette Kress** has for years managed Nvidia's Israel operations; Globes reported roughly 6,000 Israel-based employees this year (and ~3,200, 12% of the workforce, in 2023, across Yokneam, Tel Aviv, Ra'anana, Tel Hai, Kiryat and Beersheba), following the Mellanox acquisition. Some speculate this was an operation to install an Israel-friendly CEO. Corbett notes this theory "plays into the 'it's the Jews' crowd" — he reports it rather than endorsing it.
3. **The State Department/Rubio angle**, which he treats as the strongest.

### 7. Louis-Vincent Gave (UnHerd interview) — the most interesting take, per Corbett

Key points Corbett draws out:

- The intelligence had been **known for some time** (reported in Taiwanese media); hence "why now?"
- The original *New York Times* story was sourced to the **State Department**, not the FBI or CIA — a State Department angry about Nvidia's chip sales to China and locked in a "massive fight" with the Commerce Department.
- **Hillary Clinton** attacked the administration from the right, saying that if the government knows a citizen is the target of an espionage operation and he doesn't, "you owe it to him to tell him". Gave judged this as (B) cheap political point-scoring rather than (A) superior inside information — Corbett agrees.
- **China is the one bipartisan issue**: "Democrats and Republicans can't even agree on what a woman is, but they can agree together to hate China" — and the party out of power always accuses the other of being soft on China.

### 8. Rubio's fingerprints

- **Axios:** Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick received a highly classified memo in May alerting him to the affair; Rubio oversaw its drafting.
- **NYT:** Rubio informed Trump of the affair aboard Air Force One en route to the China summit in May.

Corbett's 2D-chess reading: Rubio is positioning for 2028 as the hardliner who stood up to the "China doves" such as Lutnick — who is being "thrown under the bus" and may lose his post — burnishing credentials for a platform even a Democrat could vote for.

### 9. The 3D chess layer

The space-plane story (a US intelligence report, first cited by TWZ and reported by Zero Hedge, with a Maxar satellite image) shows China's experimental space plane resembling the X-37B. Corbett connects this to his 2019 editorial "China's Suspiciously American Arsenal", listing alleged carbon copies:

- PLAN tactical laser ← US Navy Laser Weapon System
- Shenyang J-31 ← Lockheed Martin F-35B
- "Sharp Sword" UCAV ← Northrop Grumman X-47B
- Sunward SVU-200 Flying Tiger ← Northrop Grumman MQ-8 Fire Scout

Plus *Popular Mechanics* on China's CH-4B drone displaying operator information **in English**, which "begs the question" whether US/Western software is flying on Chinese military aircraft. His conclusion: the Chinese build-up has been **fostered by elements of the US military-industrial complex, government and intelligence agencies** — building the 21st-century boogeyman the way the Soviet one was built.

Supporting historical material, quoted from his essay *Escaping the Grand Chessboard* and citing historian **Anthony Sutton**, *Western Technology and Soviet Economic Development*:

- Lend-Lease transfers of atomic information and uranium to the USSR;
- Ford's 1929 technical assistance deal for the Gorky automobile plant, whose military vehicles were later used in Korea and Vietnam;
- Albert Kahn's design of the Volgograd Tractor Plant (tank production), steel from New York's McClintock Marshall Company, US engineers overseeing construction;
- the Johnson administration waiving export controls so US firms could work with Fiat on the Volgograd automobile plant in the late 1960s — at the height of the Vietnam War, when North Vietnam drew 80% of supplies from the USSR.

The 3D thesis: the real players' allegiance is not to Team West or East but "to their fellow international oligarchs" — hidden financial, economic and military actors setting the board.

### 10. The three easy steps to World War III

1. **Manufacture the crisis** using "stooges like Marco Rubio" over something known behind the scenes for a year or more, to remove a scapegoat on the 2D board.
2. **Ramp up the trade war** — wrecking any APEC rapprochement and feeding China's rare-earths retaliation.
3. **Trade war → hot war.** He attributes "when goods don't cross borders, soldiers will" to Bastiat but immediately notes a fact-check showing there is no proof Bastiat wrote it — the quote is "at best dubiously sourced" though a truism. He points to US military war-gaming of a 2027 Taiwan scenario, and raises the possibility of Cold War 2.0 ("Red Scare, Yellow Scare") versus something worse — suggesting oligarchs may increasingly regard surplus population as dispensable now that automation is advancing.

### 11. The comeback

Latest development, per the *Financial Times*: Huang is reportedly weighing a return, with a "war room" of Silicon Valley executives lobbying the White House with his support. An open letter had by Saturday morning gathered **100,000+ names**, including 5,248 self-identified current Nvidia staff and 52,233 self-identified shareholders. Musk is quoted: without Jensen, at least some critical Nvidia–supplier deals will be lost. (The transcript attributes a further remark — that the removal is "an Apple firing Steve Jobs level of idiocy from Nvidia's board" — to a name rendered as "Sweeney", which is garbled.) Corbett closes by noting factions may be fighting over the ouster, and that Huang could yet be restored.
