## Bitcoin, Blockchain, and the Surveillance Infrastructure

Patrick delivers **surprising skepticism about Bitcoin's origins and purpose**:

- He attended 2013 Congressional hearings where figures like **Chuck Schumer** praised Bitcoin's technology while promising only to pursue "bad actors"—behaviour Patrick found suspiciously uncritical for a supposed threat to central bankers.
- He notes **NSA papers on e-cash from 1996**, and that **Ripple (XRP) was founded before Bitcoin existed**.
- His "crazy thesis": **Bitcoin may have been created by intelligence services** (possibly DARPA/NSA) to familiarise the public with digital money, thereby easing transition to CBDCs. He points to the "mythological Satoshi" narrative as implausible given how few programmers worked in this space at the time.
- He highlights **tether manipulation**: the Panama Papers revealed tether and Bitfinex shared ownership, with fabricated tethers used to pump or crash Bitcoin prices. Despite this, regulators protected rather than prosecuted them—suggesting protected status.

Patrick warns that blockchain enables **tokenisation of all physical assets**—Larry Fink of BlackRock has stated desires to tag "everything in the world" with encrypted hashes. The system being built is **"decentralised centralisation"**: private companies hold siloed data (medical, travel, financial) that can be queried through interoperable protocols, circumventing constitutional restrictions on direct government surveillance.

## The Great Taking: Financial Asset Seizure

Patrick has published economic journal articles on **"The Great Taking"** from an Austrian business cycle perspective. His key findings:

- Since the 1960s, brokers have used **rehypothecation**—lending the same securities as collateral repeatedly through derivatives trades.
- With ~$30 trillion in stocks/bonds at DTCC/Euroclear, the derivatives complex may reach **$2 quadrillion** in notional exposure.
- 1990s legal changes transformed ownership: investors hold only **"securities entitlements"** (contractual claims against brokers), not actual property rights. In a general collapse, secured creditors can seize assets from pooled collateral at central securities depositories—**investors cannot sue these higher pools**.
- **Bank for International Settlements documents describe this as a "death spiral"** of margin calls and asset seizures.

Patrick argues this is the plumbing behind **"you'll own nothing and be happy"**—a engineered financial collapse could transfer all productive assets upward.

## War on Farmers and Food Control

Patrick's **48-minute film Nitrogen 2000** exposed fraudulent environmental arguments against Dutch farmers:

- **70% of Dutch national territory is farmer-owned**; the government sought to **force-purchase 35% of the country**, putting half of farmers out of business, with NGOs becoming "custodians" of state-owned land.
- The official justification: cow dung produces nitrogen that grows nettles that shade **10 "protected" orchid species**—common plants, not endangered. The minister behind the policy **could not name these species when asked**.
- Patrick identifies **land control as the real motive**: 25% of Earth's surface is grazing land; removing farmers enables consolidation. He compares this to **Stalin's dekulakisation**—eliminating independent, self-reliant populations tied to land.

He notes **synthetic food failures** (Bill Gates' "Wonder Meat" reportedly bankrupt) yet continued capital flows, suggesting market capture rather than profitability as the goal.
