# Not good in Europe

![Not good in Europe](https://storage.ghost.io/c/9c/2a/9c2aa86d-156e-419f-9a0f-38bf91aebf15/content/images/size/w2000/2026/10/It---s-Starting_-The-Fall-of-Europe.jpg)

Andrei surmises that Europe is entering a period of serious unrest, citing a wave of protests:

- Over **half a million** protesters in France; supermarkets looted, luxury boutiques smashed, public buildings set on fire, thousands arrested.
- **Milan:** rioters cleared one of Europe’s most famous squares.
- Marches in **Belgium** and **Portugal** the same weekend.
- A week earlier, a **400-year-old market in Paris** burned down.

- He rejects the official explanation (underfunded schools, leaky ceilings, missing teachers) as implausible, and sets out to examine “the more interesting theories”, ranging from fiscal collapse to deliberate social engineering.
- Stated relevance to a US audience: the same immigration debate, debt problem and surveillance technology exist in America, and Europe is framed as a **testing ground** for what comes next.

### Theory 1 — The numbers don’t work

- Economy needs more productive contributors than dependants; the host uses a restaurant analogy (retirees eating free, paid for by other diners).
- **France is the extreme case:**
  - Roughly **one-third of the economy** spent on social programmes — more than any other developed country.
  - **Pensions ≈ 14% of GDP**, second only to Italy.
  - No balanced budget since **1974** (“the last time France had a surplus was when Nixon just resigned”).
  - Debt around **€3.6 trillion**, ≈ **119% of GDP**.
  - Interest payments expected to cost around **€65 billion** this year.
- **Demographics:** ~**2.1** children per woman needed for a stable population; the **EU is at 1.34** — described as the lowest on record.
- **The proposed fix — immigration:**
  - The **UN published a report in 2000 literally titled *Replacement Migration***, calculating how many immigrants Europe would need to keep its workforce from shrinking.
  - Today about **one in four babies born in Europe has a mother born in another country**.
- A widely shared tweet (over a million views) is cited: young Europeans spent the 2010s collecting “useless degrees” the economy doesn’t pay for, just as AI arrives for entry-level jobs.
- Consequence: austerity everywhere **except defence** — “no money for you but all the money in the world for weapons”, which he links to the schools controversy.

### Theory 2 — Immigration without integration

- Attributed to a prediction by the **UAE Foreign Minister, Abdullah bin Zayed**, in **2017**: Europe would eventually produce “far more radical extremist[s] and terrorists” through indecision, political correctness, and the assumption of superior understanding of the Middle East and Islam — “pure ignorance”. He later reposted the clip with “I told you so.”
- The host’s distinction: Europe prioritised **appearing tolerant** over **integration**.
  - Integration = language, work, obeying the law, adopting basic values — and it happens because people **have no choice**, not because they want to.
  - Personal example: his own family’s arrival in the US with no English and no Russian-speaking community forced assimilation; contrast with **Brighton Beach, New York**, where one could live entirely in Russian.
- European cities formed **enclaves / “parallel societies”**, producing a generation “stuck between two worlds”, resentment, rivalry and conflict.
- The US also has enclaves (Little Italy, Chinatown, Brighton Beach) but **work forces integration** — language → job → money. A generous welfare state weakens that pressure: “apply for welfare… you just get paid to live there.”
- Late corrective measures:
  - **Denmark’s** originally named “ghetto law”, requiring young children in certain neighbourhoods to attend daycare teaching Danish language and values.
  - **Italy’s** cap on foreign students per classroom — ironically one of the things Milan students were protesting.
- Cited as long predicted: **Karl Popper’s “paradox of tolerance” (1945)** — unlimited tolerance eventually lets the intolerant take over.

### Theory 3 — Follow the incentives

- If immigration hasn’t delivered the promised economic benefit, why continue? Because it works “perfectly for the people at the very top”.
- **Beneficiary 1 — big business:** immigration lowers overall incomes and input costs. Historical precedent: West Germany’s **Gastarbeiter** programme from the 1950s (Italy, Greece, then Turkey) intended as temporary; many stayed and brought families, producing one of the world’s largest Turkish communities. In **2015** Germany took in roughly **a million asylum seekers**, cheered by the **CEO of Mercedes-Benz**, who said refugees could fuel Germany’s next economic miracle.
- **Beneficiary 2 — politicians:** new citizens become new voters. In France, polls reportedly show Muslim voters backing the **far-left party** by large margins — the same party supporting the protests.
- **Beneficiary 3 — ideologues:** **“luxury beliefs”** — positions that cost the holder nothing; politicians in Paris don’t live in the changing neighbourhoods, don’t use the overcrowded schools, don’t compete for low-wage jobs.
- **Beneficiary 4 — central planners:** chaos justifies expanded powers, surveillance and speech restrictions, which are hard to reverse.
- Framed as a **K-shaped economy**: “privatise the gains and socialise the losses”; cheap labour for owners, wage pressure for workers; moral credit for elites, consequences for neighbourhoods; votes for parties, crime for residents — “heads I win, tails you lose.”
- Conclusion: not incompetence — **no incentive for those in power to fix it.**

### Theory 4 — Late-stage capitalism (credited to Simon Dixon)

- Three power blocs: the **financial-industrial complex**, the **military-industrial complex** and the **technological-industrial complex** (referred to in the transcript as the FIC, MIC and TIC).
- The forever-war profit model in the Middle East is ending (AI and robots), so capital is rotating into defence: in **2025 NATO countries agreed to raise defence spending from 2% to 5% of GDP by 2035**, paid for by squeezing pensions, schools and other spending.
- **Rheinmetall** (described as Germany’s largest weapons manufacturer): share price around **$100** the day before Russia invaded Ukraine, around **$1,000** three years later — a 10x return.
- **Surveillance:** before the **2024 Paris Olympics**, France became the **first EU country to legalise AI-powered video surveillance** (cameras analysing movement and “suspicious behaviour”). Presented as temporary, it was extended, with the government wanting it to continue until **2030**.
- **Manufactured unrest:** Dixon allegedly argues the civil unrest itself was created deliberately, with suspicious-looking footage circulating on X. Precedent offered: **Operation Gladio** — NATO’s covert Cold War stay-behind armies in Western Europe; the **Italian Prime Minister publicly admitted it in 1990** and the **European Parliament condemned it that year**; Italian investigations linked some involved to far-right terrorists behind bombings blamed on the left, during the **“strategy of tension”** — scare the public into demanding more security and control. The host explicitly labels the contemporary claim **speculation with no public evidence**.
- **The repricing event:** geopolitical transition (US-led order → regional powers) requires a crash; ordinary people sell assets at a loss, “the people at the top” buy them. Dixon calls this **“pricing the average person out of ownership”** and moving them into a **lease-based economy — “you rent everything and you own nothing”**. The **Strait of Hormuz** and civil unrest are said to be central to this.
- Summary: “a controlled, for-profit demolition” — war profits weapons makers financed by banks; fear justifies surveillance for tech firms; the crash transfers assets to big players and losses to taxpayers.

### Theory 5 — The “Agenda 2030 endgame”

- Referencing **Bret Weinstein**, an evolutionary biologist, who argues: “We are useless eaters from the point of view of our overlords.” Elites need ordinary people only while they “can’t get their plumbing to work”; once AI and humanoid robots can do every job better and cheaper, there is nothing stopping a decision that we are “unnecessary”.
- The immigration crisis is framed as replacing a population attached to the idea of rights with people without that history.
- Warning to Gen Z: act now, because at scale “you can be debunked in an instant” or have evidence fabricated against you — “that’s the end of our ability to fight.”
- Corroborating anecdote: writer **Douglas Rushkoff**, expecting to speak about investing, found himself advising **five billionaires on doomsday bunkers** — including “how do I maintain control of my Navy SEAL security force after my Bitcoin’s not worth anything”.
- Framing: “Agenda 2030 is the public version… the nice-sounding one”; the “real version” is more intentional.

### Andrei's personal conclusion

- “I think there’s truth in all of them.”
- Rejects the charge of fear-mongering, describing what he calls the “culling of the herd” as already in progress:
  - **Food that makes us sicker** because sick people are profitable → forever pharmaceutical customers.
  - **A failing education system**; division “on every front”, amplified by curating algorithms.
  - **Civil strife as the cheapest revenue stream** — keeping people fighting each other instead of the real problem.
  - **Financial repression and inflation** eroding savings while owners of real resources get richer — and fewer children being born.
- Says “there is in fact a shadowy room full of elites” — **the World Economic Forum in Davos** — but adds that even without a coordinating room, the outcome is explicable by incentive alone: **Charlie Munger’s “Show me the incentive and I’ll show you the outcome.”**
- Philosophical note: it is “almost human nature… to systematically enslave and subordinate ourselves”; capitalism plus extraction reaches its logical conclusion in a handful of winners controlling most resources.

### His practical prescriptions (the “take back control” section)

- **Health:** cook your own food, move your body, read labels — “none of them can sell you a lifetime of prescriptions if you don’t need them.”
- **Education:** educate yourself and your family; “everything you need to learn is free.”
- **Attention:** the algorithm wants you “scared, angry and divided”; noticing that and closing the app reclaims control.
- **Division:** don’t fall for basic narratives.
- **Ownership:** if the plan is “you’ll own nothing and be happy”, then “the most rebellious thing I can do is own something” — assets that can’t be printed, inflated away or frozen.
- Closing line: know there is a game, and know how to play it.
