## The mechanism: "Connect and Personalise"

- A feature added to store payment devices roughly a year before the email. It pulls up a customer's devices, AppleCare+ status and existing paid services, ostensibly to help recommend accessories ("drive accessory attach", in Apple's language).
- Data is stored on the device when the customer shares it, and allegedly deleted after the transaction ends or is cancelled.
- The same interface contains a **trials sign-up screen**: the employee selects an offer and approves it on a single screen. The trial starts immediately and the customer is billed when it ends. **No input is required from the customer** beyond an "accept" button.
- Offers cited include Fitness+, News+ and "Creator Studio", plus Apple One bundles. Rossmann's aside: "Remember when you just bought Final Cut once? You can now pay a low price forever."
- The emailer's core allegation: **there is zero friction preventing a specialist from signing someone up without telling them.**

## Why it happens: metric pressure

- Management obsession with metrics described as worse than ever, and a constant throughout the emailer's tenure.
- Key sales metric: **the percentage of transactions that contain services**. Seasonal staff are reportedly denied permanent roles solely for missing it; staff answer "how are you?" with their numbers for the day.
- Service attach reportedly averages **60–80% across all customer interactions** at the emailer's store — i.e. up to 80% of customers enrolled in trials of things they likely won't use, don't know about, and therefore won't cancel.
- The emailer, a top performer on AppleCare+, accessories and business accounts, says honest pitching of services is **rejected nearly 100% of the time** — so the store figures "don't add up".
- On raising the frictionless design with a manager, the response was dismissive: *"Well, it's obviously the expectation that we're not doing that."*

## Consent and disclosure

Asked by Rossmann why a salesperson's screen offers "Decline/Agree":

- The buttons are **by design meant for the customer**, but customers often never see the screen because nothing stops the employee pressing accept.
- Employees have reportedly described trials to customers **as a gift**, treating a nod or half-hearted "okay" as consent.
- **There is never a disclosure that the customer will eventually be charged.**

## Escalation and alleged cover

- Probing high performers, the emailer found several who admitted — one who bragged — about signing people up without consent.
- Apple's **Business Conduct team refused to respond without individual names**, showing it will only handle cases individually rather than address the systemic issue.
- Conversations with staff at other stores confirmed it as "an unspoken issue" there too; searching **r/AppleEmployees** showed it to be nationwide.
- Customers have returned months later to complain, but staff believe these cases aren't traced back to the individual employee — helped by most trials running three months, and by many customers not knowing how to cancel at all.
- The emailer's framing: Apple has built a system of **plausible deniability**, while top-down orders create the pressure to fudge numbers through fraudulent sign-ups, at the risk of employees' livelihoods. Nobody at store level will take the blame because so many are implicated.
- Motive asserted: **Apple services are the largest portion of Apple's revenue growth**, so no visible action is taken.
- Conclusion: "I can't work for a place that enables this type of shady criminal behavior… [watching] the energizing creative culture wither into a soulless corporate husk."

## Rossmann's unanswered questions

1. Whether this happens at stores beyond those of the few people who have contacted him.
2. Whether the customer receives an email or notification when the trial starts **explicitly stating the billing date**.
3. Whether store or regional management knows.
4. What Apple says if asked to comment.

## What he is asking whistleblowers to send

1. Photos of the Connect and Personalise screens — especially any step requiring the **customer's own phone**, and any screen where an employee can accept on the customer's behalf.
2. What the customer receives the moment "accept" is hit — email, receipt line, notification, even with details redacted.
3. Any written target or tracking for service attach: scorecards, dashboards, coaching notes, performance reviews, improvement plans, team emails, internal chat posts (Zulip or Slack), and any policy pushing it.
4. Training material or talking points for presenting trials — anything calling them a gift, omitting the charge, or allowing staff not to state plainly that billing starts in three months.
5. What happens when the issue is raised with management or Business Conduct — replies, or the lack of them.
6. Evidence from seasonal or part-time staff denied a job because of this metric.
7. When the feature first appeared, to build a timeline.
8. Contact: the email address shown on screen.
