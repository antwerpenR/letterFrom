# Treaty of Versailles

# Treaty of Versailles

The Treaty of Versailles, signed on June 28, 1919, was a peace treaty that formally ended World War I and imposed harsh penalties on Germany. The treaty was negotiated by the Allied powers, primarily represented by the "Big Three" - David Lloyd George (Britain), Georges Clemenceau (France), and Woodrow Wilson (USA)[1](https://www.historyhit.com/the-key-points-of-the-treaty-of-versailles/?ref=rogerprice.me).

Key provisions of the Treaty of Versailles included:

1. Territorial changes: Germany lost 13% of its European territory, including Alsace-Lorraine to France, parts of Prussia to Poland, and various other regions to neighboring countries[2](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/treaty-of-versailles?ref=rogerprice.me)[3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles?ref=rogerprice.me).
2. Military restrictions: Germany's military was severely limited, with its army restricted to 100,000 men, navy reduced to six battleships, and air force completely prohibited[1](https://www.historyhit.com/the-key-points-of-the-treaty-of-versailles/?ref=rogerprice.me)[4](https://www.bbc.co.uk/bitesize/articles/zxjg3j6?ref=rogerprice.me).
3. Reparations: Germany was required to pay substantial war reparations, initially set at 6.6 billion pounds[4](https://www.bbc.co.uk/bitesize/articles/zxjg3j6?ref=rogerprice.me).
4. War guilt clause: Article 231 forced Germany to accept full responsibility for starting World War I[2](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/treaty-of-versailles?ref=rogerprice.me).
5. Demilitarization: The Rhineland was to be demilitarized, with all fortifications in the region demolished[3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles?ref=rogerprice.me).
6. Colonial losses: Germany was stripped of all its overseas colonies, which became League of Nations mandates[3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles?ref=rogerprice.me).
7. International organizations: The treaty established the League of Nations, although Germany was initially excluded from joining[1](https://www.historyhit.com/the-key-points-of-the-treaty-of-versailles/?ref=rogerprice.me)[5](https://www.bbc.co.uk/bitesize/guides/zbg4t39/revision/3?ref=rogerprice.me).
8. Economic restrictions: Germany faced various economic limitations, including temporary control of the Saar region by France[5](https://www.bbc.co.uk/bitesize/guides/zbg4t39/revision/3?ref=rogerprice.me).

The Treaty of Versailles was met with shock and disbelief in Germany, as its terms were considered extremely punitive[5](https://www.bbc.co.uk/bitesize/guides/zbg4t39/revision/3?ref=rogerprice.me). The harsh conditions imposed by the treaty would later contribute to political and economic instability in Germany, which some historians argue played a role in the rise of Nazi Germany and the outbreak of World War II.
