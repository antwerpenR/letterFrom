Voidzilla argues that the biggest China-related risk to Nvidia was not a Chinese AI model such as DeepSeek or GLM, but a person: **Alice Huang**, described as a Chinese-born executive at Singapore-based chip buyer **Megaspeed**, with whom CEO **Jensen Huang** had an affair — and whom US intelligence has reportedly identified as a Chinese spy. The presenter's core claim is that the affair is the small part of the story; the real issue is that Nvidia spent years telling investors and regulators that it was "100% out of China" while allegedly looking away from billions of dollars of GPUs being funnelled to China through Southeast Asian intermediaries.

## Significant claims

**1. The "100% out of China" line is the crux**  
The presenter argues Nvidia's public position — zero China exposure — was achieved on paper only, with China demand continuing to be served through Southeast Asian intermediaries. Culper allegedly puts the real figure at **over 20% of Nvidia's compute revenue** still China-driven via diversion.

**2. The Megaspeed denials were "lawyered", not honest**  
The presenter seizes on Nvidia's statement that Megaspeed had "no China shareholders" and calls it "the most lawyered answer ever". His argument: Megaspeed's Malaysian subsidiary **Speed Matrix Sdn Bhd** carries liabilities and obligations to **Apex Enterprise Solutions**, tied to Alibaba. So Megaspeed may have no Chinese *shareholders* while being financially bound to a Chinese company. His point is that producing such a technically-true-but-misleading statement requires knowing the underlying facts.

**3. The dummy-server pattern was visible for months**  
The indictment (dummy servers faking audits), then Bain's eviction of Megaspeed for the same practice, are presented as proof that the industry could see what Nvidia claimed it could not.

**4. Culper flagged more than Megaspeed**  
The report allegedly notes:

- Megaspeed's website is nearly identical to a Chinese company, **CM AI**;
- A second entity, **Novagate**, has a website almost identical to Megaspeed's — described as "another Megaspeed offshoot" with an overlapping director;
- Alice Huang, reportedly removed from Megaspeed, was allegedly still a director and one of the top three people at the company.

**5. The conflict of interest is structural, not personal**  
The video plays Jensen Huang arguing that Chinese open-source models should run on the American tech stack and that "victimising them, turning them into an enemy… likely isn't the best answer". The presenter frames this as a business incentive to keep selling into China regardless of controls.

**6. The "he took accountability" defence is rejected**  
The presenter's sharpest criticism: days before admitting the affair, Huang was telling interviewers his interactions with Alice Huang were "typical of those with Chinese customers and executives" and that Nvidia had "studied all their deployments and counted every one". Only after the *FT* story did the admission come. Therefore the apology was compelled, not volunteered.

**7. The board investigation is pre-judged**  
He quotes the board's statement — "while the board has no reason to believe that either national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised" — and argues that declaring no expectation of wrongdoing at the *start* of an inquiry signals a foregone conclusion.

**8. Why it matters for markets**  
Nvidia fell **4.5%** on the day, and the presenter stresses the company is roughly **6% of the S&P 500** — making an integrity question at one firm a systemic market question. He also cites **Bill Ackman** ("He's an incredible human. Even incredible humans make mistakes… We should all support him and his family") as an example of the "move on" reaction he rejects.

## Fact-check notes

The broad story is real and widely reported, but some video details are imprecise:

| Video claim | Independent reporting |
| --- | --- |
| Affair, spy finding, board inquiry | Confirmed: US intelligence identified Alice Huang as a Chinese spy recruited to target Jensen Huang; Nvidia opened an investigation after he acknowledged the relationship [1] [2] |
| Culper report, 13 May 2026, *"The China Problem"*, short position | Confirmed; Culper alleged over 20% of Nvidia's FY2026 compute revenue was still China-driven via Southeast Asian intermediaries and called Novagate a Megaspeed offshoot [3] [4] |
| "Bainbridge's data centre" dropped Megaspeed | Appears to be **Bain's** (Bain Capital) data-centre arm dropping Megaspeed as a tenant in Malaysia [5] |
| Bloomberg: $4.6bn / 136,000 GPUs | *Bloomberg*'s December 2025 piece exists, but note it also stated it found **no evidence** that Megaspeed's chips had been diverted; other outlets put Megaspeed's purchases higher (~$4.8bn, with ~$7.5bn routed over time) [6] [7] [8] |
| SDNY indictment of "some Chinese people" over $2.5bn | Related prosecutions were reported in March 2026 against people tied to a US server maker accused of diverting billions in Nvidia-equipped equipment to China [9] |

**Caveats the video does not fully carry:** Megaspeed denies wrongdoing and is under investigation rather than convicted [10] [11]; the "20%-plus China revenue" figure is a short-seller's allegation, not an audited fact; and the inference that Jensen Huang knowingly enabled diversion is the presenter's interpretation, not an established finding.
