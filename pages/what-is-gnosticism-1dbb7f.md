## Key Points

### 1. Sources and the Nag Hammadi discovery

- For most of history, the only sources on the Gnostics were hostile "heresiologists" such as Irenaeus (writing c. 180 CE).
- The 1945 discovery of the **Nag Hammadi library** in Egypt uncovered Coptic texts written by these groups themselves, revolutionising scholarship.
- Irenaeus's polemical reports turn out to align reasonably well with the Gnostics' own writings, so he remains a relatively reliable — if biased — source.

### 2. "Gnosticism" is a contested category

- The word derives from Greek **gnosis** — intuitive, spiritual knowledge, not everyday information. Broadly used, it has covered Sufis, Hermeticists, even Clement of Alexandria.
- Three scholarly approaches:
  - **Universalist**: Gnosticism as a wide ancient movement including Valentinians, Marcionites, Mandaeans, Manichaeans and Hermeticists.
  - **Sceptical**: Gnosticism never existed — it is a modern, anachronistic construct.
  - **Middle position** (David Brakke, followed in this video): a narrow definition identifying Gnosticism with the Sethian school.

### 3. Early Christianity was diverse, not "Orthodoxy plus heresies"

- The old model of one true church from which sects diverged has been abandoned.
- **Andrew Henry (Religion for Breakfast)** contributes a segment: Athanasius's 367 CE letter fixing the 27-book canon was written from a *precarious* position, not a dominant one (per Brakke's research). Competing Christianities thrived side by side — e.g. Valentinians formed study circles around charismatic teachers rather than parish structures.
- **Epiphanius of Cyprus** (*Panarion*) accused Gnostics of ritual cannibalism — lurid fabrications. **David Frankfurter** notes that accusing rivals of ritualised sex or cannibalism was a classic strategy to ostracise opponents.
- These groups were not underground: Tertullian records that **Valentinus nearly became bishop of Rome**; Valentinians attended ordinary churches.
- Scholars now speak of **varieties of early Christianity** rather than an Orthodoxy-vs-heresy binary.

### 4. Core Sethian Gnostic myth

Drawn chiefly from the **Secret Book of John (Apocryphon of John)**, plus *Zostrianos*, *Hypostasis of the Archons*, *Zoroaster* and the later-discovered **Gospel of Judas**:

- At the apex is the unknowable ultimate God — the **One/Monad**, "the invisible Spirit", described apophatically (unnameable, immeasurable, eternal).
- From its self-contemplation emanates the **Pleroma** (fullness) of divine **aeons**, often paired male–female, totalling around 24 — aeons such as Truth, Mind, Life and above all **Wisdom (Sophia)**.
- The first emanation is **Barbelo** (Forethought), the most consistently attested figure across texts. With the invisible Spirit she begets the **Self-Originate (Christ)**, forming a triad of Father, Mother and Son.
- **Sophia's fall**: she creates without her partner's or the Spirit's consent, producing a misshapen pseudo-divine being — **Yaldabaoth** (also Saklas or Iao), snake-bodied, lion-faced — hidden in a cloud.
- Yaldabaoth, ignorant of the true Pleroma, creates the **material world**, believing himself the only god. Hence the God of the Hebrew Bible is *not* the true God — a radical rereading. His jealous boast "there is no other God apart from me" paradoxically gives him away.
- Adam receives a **divine spark** when Yaldabaoth is tricked into breathing his spirit into him.
- **Seth**, conceived purely (unlike Cain and Abel), is the bearer of gnosis — the ancestor of the Gnostics, who call themselves the **"seed of Seth"**.
- The **serpent** is a helpful messenger from the Pleroma, not a deceiver — the mainstream reading inverted.

### 5. Christology and salvation

- The Gnostics were **Docetists**: Jesus only *seemed* to have a material body and did not truly suffer on the cross.
- Jesus saves not by his death but by **teaching gnosis** — knowledge of humanity's true identity and divine home, freeing us from the material prison.

### 6. Practices

- A distinctive **baptism involving the "five seals"** (exact meaning uncertain), possibly repeated at successive spiritual stages.
- **Ascent practices**: contemplation of progressively higher aeons, up to Barbelo and, imperfectly, the invisible Spirit itself; combined with asceticism and study of mystical-philosophical works.
- They **rejected the Eucharist** as foolish — mocked in the *Gospel of Judas*, where Judas alone recognises Jesus's true origin ("the immortal realm of Barbelo").

### 7. Related movements

- **Valentinians** (after Valentinus of Alexandria): borrowed and simplified the Gnostic myth in more Christian terms; less world-negating; blended into ordinary congregations; Irenaeus's chief target. Texts: *Gospel of Truth*, *Tripartite Tractate*.
- **Marcionites**: shared the demiurge idea, but their creator was "unrelentingly just" rather than evil; the highest God is "the Stranger"; Marcion rejected the Old Testament entirely (unlike the Gnostics, who kept it for teaching) and produced the first attempted New Testament canon.
- Later echoes: **Manichaeism**, **Mandaeism**, and possibly the medieval **Cathars**; the video closes by noting modern revivals of interest and even self-identified Gnostics today.
