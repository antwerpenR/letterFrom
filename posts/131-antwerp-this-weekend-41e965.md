🇧🇪 We are now in Belgium for a bit. We travelled down to Dublin Friday last week on the bus - so as promised here is an update on the travel experience. *Dublin Express* coach continues to be our preferred choice - they currently have a separate bus for the airport and one that only goes to Dublin City so there was plenty of space, these buses are new and the drivers are friendly.

In Terminal 2 it is now all self-checkin. You need to be careful to check the right suitcases with each boarding pass or you will end up having to pay extra or being stuck with missing baggage allocation and having to seek out manual intervention to resolve the mess. Of course this is unfriendly, difficult and slow. I can imagine that all this software is built via "tickets" and contractor programmers write tests to demonstrate that their program works - nobody tests the edge cases, tries to break it or tests for usability.

Airport security remains the useless psyop that it always was - this is just *obedience training* for the masses and a *ritual* that *they require you to perform.* The purpose is to train you to accept ridiculous constraints as normal and to obey instructions from so-called *authority*. Pro-traveller tip for Terminal 2 is to head left and keep left and you will eventually end up beside the Fast Track. If there is no agent on guard you might *accidentally* walk into the *Fast Track* - and if there is an agent there they may well ask "*are you on your own*" and if you answer "yes" they will let you go through too.

Arrival in Brussels Zaventem was a *dreadful experience*. Our luggage came in on band 8 behind two other flights, the first of which was from Africa - so all the passengers were stuck in passport control and the band was completely full and no more luggage was coming out. Of course the other luggage bands around us were empty - this would seem to be negligence or incompetence, if not nefarious or malicious planning by the airport. V and I explained to those around us that they needed to make space on the band but only a couple of people could be bothered to help - the rest just stood around like cabbages. So we had a long delay there and missed our planned train connection. Welcome to Belgium!

After all that we had a great week here, visiting Oostende where we even went to the races, bike to Veurne and train to Bruges in addition to plenty of pleasant walks along the beach. We have been staying with V's brother and his partner - thanks for all the hospitality! ### Anna's Archive

Recall our discussion last week on the evil of *Audible* and *disappearing audiobooks*. Well that cancer is also present in *Amazon* book services where e-books are just as likely to disappear from your *Kindle* (or even be altered). Thankfully there is also a solution to that too. This is an even easier project than last week's one - since you do not need to run any software - although you are welcome to do so and this is one way to help the project.

*Anna's Archiv*e preserves books, papers, comics, magazines and more by bringing these materials from various [shadow libraries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_library?ref=rogerprice.me), official libraries, and other collections *together in one place*. All this data is preserved forever by making it easy to duplicate it in bulk — using torrents — resulting in many copies around the world. Listen below as Gabriel explains - you are welcome to join and support this initiative in various ways.

For those who have questions about *copyright* you should listen carefully to their discussion; this should also be rather familiar territory to all *Austrian Economists* and anyone familiar with Saifedean's [*Principles of Economics*](https://rogerprice.me/at-the-tennis-club/#book-of-the-week).

## 🤔 Closing Thoughts

This week I am closing out with a comparison of two rather important and influential *spiritual tradition*s. These are things that are not commonly discussed and knowing them will likely give you many additional insights and much wisdom.

*Hermeticism* and *Gnosticism* share a common emphasis on *knowledge* and the *spiritual journey*, they diverge significantly in their cosmological views, attitudes toward the material world, and approaches to salvation. One of the most significant differences lies in their *cosmological frameworks*.

*Gnosticism* typically posits a *dualistic worldview*, where the material world is seen as a flawed or evil creation of a lesser deity, often referred to as the *Demiurge*. This belief leads to a negative perception of the physical realm, which is viewed as a prison for the divine spark within humanity.

In contrast, *Hermeticism* adopts a more *monistic approach*, viewing the material world as a *sacred manifestation of the divine*. Hermetic texts often describe creation as a process of emanation from *the One*, where the material realm is not inherently evil but a necessary aspect of the divine plan.

### Hermeticism and the Kybalion

Hermeticism is an esoteric tradition that encompasses a variety of philosophical and spiritual teachings attributed to *Hermes Trismegistu*s. Its seven core principles reflect a synthesis of ancient wisdom, focusing on the relationship between the divine, the cosmos, and humanity. The most accessible explainer is *The Kybalion*.

### So what is Gnosticism and why now?

This is rather good interview that I came across this week. Gordon White is an interesting character and whilst I do not agree with everything he says he has many interesting, pertinent and relevant perspectives. Dan Waites, the interviewer does an excellent job interviewing and summarising.

Gnosticism, a collection of sects and beliefs that thrived in the centuries after Christ, was suppressed by the Church around the 4th century. The Gnostics believed in *two gods* - the transcendent supreme *Monad*, which is the *ultimate creator*, and the *Demiurge*, an evil or incompetent deity that created the material world. They believed this world was evil, akin to a prison - but that knowledge of the higher reality, gnosis, could set them free.   
  
Gnosticism lay dormant for centuries - but in our time, it has seemingly returned - so why is that?
