## Book of the week

In recent weeks I have been discussing with many folks who are wakening up to what is happening and realising that change is necessary. Making the change can be easier than you think - *it is under your control*.

This is not new wisdom - some of you may have suspected as much and this wisdom was clearly presented in Neville Goddard's *book of 1949*. It links with our recent discussions on [*Gateway Process*](https://rogerprice.me/145-back-in-bangkok/#:~:text=are%20at%20hand-,%F0%9F%A4%94%20Closing%20Thoughts,-I%20mentioned%20above), visualisation and [dreams](https://rogerprice.me/25-a-week-in-antwerp/#:~:text=%F0%9F%98%B4%20Experimenting%20%2D%20why%20not?) - in addition to Jason's book: [Awaken the Immortal within](https://rogerprice.me/105-the-great-taking/#%F0%9F%A4%94-closing-thoughts) and our [Holographic Universe](https://rogerprice.me/170-family-in-town/#:~:text=see%20it%20too-,%F0%9F%A4%94%20Closing%20Thoughts,-To%20close%20out) discussion.

*Neville's* *book* suggests that our world as we experience it is but a projection of a higher, fourth-dimensional reality—the world of your imagination, where *all future possibilities already exist*. To change your life you don't have to fight external circumstances; *you simply need to select a new reality from within*.

The key is to **assume the feeling of your wish already fulfilled**.

The method is straightforward: in a relaxed, meditative state, create a short mental scene that *implies* your goal is accomplished. Don't just watch it; feel it with all your senses until it seems completely real. By persisting in this new inner state, you impress it upon the world and your assumption will *inevitably* harden into fact.

The ultimate message is that *you are the creator of your destiny*. Stop trying to change the outside world and start by changing the state within your own wonderful human imagination. If you are still skeptical, try this 30 minute explainer - it summarises and presents well the key concepts of the book.

For those who are skeptical I can share that I have personal experience of this working over decades. Some of you will recall me from my professional days when I undertook the biggest and most challenging projects that others would not touch. In each of these I only imagined myself succeeding and achieving the goals. The universe really does arrange "reality" to match. A few other examples include my *choice of university*, my *move to Belgium* and *then to Hong Kong* as well as *my consulting projects* and *what V and I are doing now*.

I have also shared with you that a lot of this resonates with the [*7 Habits of Highly Effective people*](https://rogerprice.me/choices-you-de/#the-7-habits-of-highly-effective-people) - in particular that "a*ll things are created twice*". Neville cites many other references.

### The big long

Former Wall Street options trader Scott (@DarkSide2030\_) explains his "*Big Long*" thesis - how Bitcoin's unique properties as a digital bearer asset will trigger an unprecedented derivatives crisis. Unlike gold or traditional assets, Bitcoin *can be verified on-chain and taken into self-custody within hours*, creating impossible dynamics for Wall Street's rehypothecation schemes. As institutions adopt Bitcoin and derivatives markets expand, the system faces a liquidity crunch when investors flee to self-custody during the next financial crisis. This feedback loop could cause systemic collapse as banks struggle with counterparty risk while Bitcoin becomes increasingly scarce and unavailable for borrowing.

### Being well organised

Some of you have been asking me about how I keep on top of all my projects and activities. Many of you likely do have a ToDo list of some sort and there has been a lot of pressure for people to put these online in tools.

For me, a good paper list works best and I'm happy to share an interesting technique that you may well want to try - [The Resistance Zero System](http://markforster.squarespace.com/blog/2022/6/14/resistance-how-to-make-the-most-of-it-the-resistance-zero-sy.html?ref=rogerprice.me). Ben explains it in his video below with some real life examples:

> [!image]
> [![Click for Ben's very pratical guide to this useful system](https://storage.ghost.io/c/9c/2a/9c2aa86d-156e-419f-9a0f-38bf91aebf15/content/images/2025/07/image-6.png)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tsgj1\_OwhPs&ref=rogerprice.me)
> Click for Ben's very pratical guide to this useful system

This really does work extremely well. Essential tips are:  
- be sure to review every item each day, starting from the bottom of the list  
- items that have been static for too long likely need closing by re-phrasing and splitting  
- when you near 3/4 page with closed items it's good to copy open items to a new page and cointinue there. This may also prompt you to rephrase and split some items.

How about you? What techniques are useful for you? I do also have my scheduled reminders in *Obsidian* - with a simple *dataView* that I wrote. This ensures that I do not forget about items that are scheduled in the future.
