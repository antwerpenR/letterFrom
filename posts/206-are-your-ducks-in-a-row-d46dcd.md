### Walking the talk - running on Linux

I have long recommended that you have and use a Linux computer - whatever you do you need to *STOP using Windoze*. Apple Mac computers are better but Linux is the best and the most private and best suited to Sovereign Individuals.

This week I am walking the talk and I have been using Linux as my daily computer - including to produce this newsletter. Focusing 100% on using the machine for everything has forced me to get everything installed and fully working on it. This has been a most interesting and educational exercise. Happy to share some tips below:

*Brave Browser* just works - on Linux as well as everywhere else. You can synchronise settings between machines if you need. This gives you access to all the services that you use every day - including self hosted ones like photos using Immich.

[*LocalSend*](https://localsend.org/?ref=rogerprice.me) is the tool that you need to give you the feeling of an ecosystem with your other computers and devices. You can run it on Android/GrapheneOS and iOS as well as Mac and it simply enables exchanging everything from clipboard to files between all devices - somewhat like Airdrop (on Apple).

*Signal and Session* messengers both work perfectly on Linux - you just need to do the initial setup and you will be all set with your ongoing discussions available on all devices.

[*Flameshot*](https://flameshot.org/?ref=rogerprice.me) is the tool that you need for screen capture and image clipping. It has all capabilities that you need and you can configure it just the way you like.

Multiple Workspaces is a *must-use* feature. This makes it simple to have all apps related to a specific task onscreen and separate from everything else on your computer - much easier to concentrate and focus.

To save local copies of videos from YouTube and other services - [*yt-dlp*](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp?ref=rogerprice.me) is the go-to utility. Be sure to use the latest version [from github](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp?ref=rogerprice.me) since the empire is continually trying to hamper making downloads. BTW - if you are on Mac, *yt-dlp* works great but [*Downie*](https://software.charliemonroe.net/downie/?ref=rogerprice.me) (a commercial app is even better - for example it automatically downloads transcripts when available.

[*SyncThing*](https://syncthing.net/?ref=rogerprice.me) magically ensures that key folders remain synchronised on all devices. For Backup you will want to use [*Pika Backup*](https://github.com/pika-backup?ref=rogerprice.me) to make remote backups and [*Timeshift*](https://github.com/linuxmint/timeshift?ref=rogerprice.me) to have local snapshots that allow recovery in case of installation or upgrade issues.

In many respects, the experience on Linux is even better than that on Mac. Yes, Mac is rather smooth and extremely well integrated with Apple devices but Linux is fast and responsive and you can configure it to work exactly as you like. It encourages you to optimise operations - for example with scripts and automation. This is great fun and it makes many things easier and faster; *Kagi* with *Kimi 2* is your friend for this.
