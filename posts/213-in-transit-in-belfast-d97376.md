### Book of the week

In recent weeks I really enjoyed reading Mark Gober’s *Upside Down Cosmos*. It is a provocative challenge to mainstream cosmology. Gober questions the Big Bang, heliocentrism and the globe model itself, arguing that the prevailing scientific narrative is an “upside-down” deception intended to diminish humanity’s sense of significance and purpose.

The book is structured in three parts: an examination of the current model and its inconsistencies; evidence suggesting a stationary Earth; and an exploration of the philosophical and spiritual implications of a purposeful, contained cosmos.

Part one questions the accepted model of the universe, pointing out unexplained scientific issues and assumptions. It discusses the *Copernican Principle*, which moved Earth from the center of everything to just a small part of a vast, empty space. It also critiques the *Big Bang* theory, explaining that concepts like *Dark Matter* and *Dark Energy* are used because current math doesn't match what we see and mainstream simply ignores all the evidence.

Part two presents evidence against the spinning globe idea, arguing for a stationary Earth. It highlights how experiments over long distances of water haven't shown the expected curve of a globe and how historical experiments, like the *Michelson-Morley experiment* can be interpreted to prove Earth doesn't move. Gober proposes that *density* and *electromagnetism*, rather than gravity, better explain why things fall. He also explores ancient ideas of a contained Earth, possibly under a dome or *firmament*.

Part three connects this alternative view to consciousness, suggesting that if Earth is a special, stationary place, it implies a Creator and a purpose for life. Gober argues the mainstream model leads to *nihilism* (the belief that life is meaningless), while his model points to spiritual significance.
