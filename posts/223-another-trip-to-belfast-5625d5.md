🇪🇸 🇬🇧 We are back in Belfast again - another unplanned trip. Unfortunately a family member has had an injury and we are here to help ensure that proper support is organised for the recuperation. Thanks to all the family friends who are also helping and supporting.

Compared to the quality of healthcare that V and I have personally experienced around the world, the service here can only be described as *abominable* - it cannot be acceptable to say "*we do not do personal care or cleaning*" to patients who are in pain to move have spent more than a day in *Accident & Emergency* department and spent the night on a trolley in the corridor. I have another friend who is experiencing something similar with a family member as well.

There are a few caring individuals in the staff but they are extinguished by the overwhelming bureaucracy of the system that treats people like tickets to be progressed through various SOPs, requiring them to be glued to their phones and computer terminals like some sort of perverse social media game. The entire system has been dehumanised. To those who will *defend NHS,* I can only say "wake up and get real".

It has been a busy week and there is still plenty to do. We are still here a few more days. The recovery will take time and we do plan to be back again in a few weeks for a longer stay during the summer time.

One of the benefits of our stage of life is that we can be flexible. I will also take every opportunity to do what is important for us every day. Drawing on the work of *Professor Samuel Cohn, Dr Scott and Dr Duncan*, Dr Moss challenges the traditional contagion theory of historical plagues, noting a strong correlation between plague sites and mining locations in Britain. She also discusses *research by Carlo Brogna et al. identifying toxin-like peptides in COVID-19 victims* and *questions the transmission narratives of the Spanish Flu*based on [experiments by Dr Rosenau](https://www.ggarchives.com/Influenza/TheRosenauExperiment-1918-1919.html?ref=rogerprice.me).

Beyond that she also highlights research demonstrating that the so-called plagues were not caused by rats or fleas, *rather most likely by poisoning of water supplies*. She cites many pieces of evidence for this, not least the historical references to smoking and alcohol providing resistance to and recovery from such poisoning.

She, like me and many others, noticed that smokers were remarkably unlikely to be affected by the recent Covid plandemic.
