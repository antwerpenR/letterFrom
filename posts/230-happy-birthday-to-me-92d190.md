### Browser update

You should be aware that [*Brave Browser*](https://rogerprice.me/229-after-the-twelfth/) has long been my go-to recommendation. It is a privacy-oriented fork of Chrome and they make a point of blocking ads everywhere, including YouTube. They have a number of other interesting features like blocking and denying all those annoying Cookie Notifications - you just never see them any more. Nice.

Recently I have noticed that Brave struggles to start YouTube videos - you often have to reload the page to get it going. This, among other things prompted me to look at [*Zen Browser*](https://zen-browser.app/?ref=rogerprice.me) which promises a [*Calmer Internet*](https://zen-browser.app/?ref=rogerprice.me). It is a Firefox fork and I have to say it is what Firefox could have been now, if they had not lost their way. I guess they had the advantage of no legacy base and they could just make a browser as it should be. You can easily run this alongside your existing browser for some time to give it a try.

I highly recommend that you install *Zen Browser* (works on Mac and Linux, and even Windoze). You must install uBlock Origin ad-blocker and the best experience is with *Vertical Tabs* (on the side) and do make use of *Spaces* to group and separate areas of activity. This setup perfectly runs YouTube videos without any adverts or glitches - what are you waiting for?

Happy to hear your feedback and recommendations- what works for you?

### Many ways to pay

We covered quite a few interesting topics in our Bitcoiner Meetup. Among them was sharing our findings and recommendations for tools that enable and facilitate using Bitcoin as money - both directly and indirectly.

#### Directly

To make payments directly in [Bitcoin](https://www.bitrefill.com/gb/en/?ref=rogerprice.me), the best way is with ***Lightning***. It works reliably now and it is easy to get started. There are many excellent wallets - [*Blitz*](https://blitz-wallet.com/?ref=rogerprice.me) (of El Salvador fame) and [*Wallet Of Satoshi*](https://www.walletofsatoshi.com/?ref=rogerprice.me) are excellent to start. Use these for your first payments. Consider them like your cash wallet - don't keep more than a hundred or so pounds/dollars. For larger amounts (thousands) [*Phoenix*](https://phoenix.acinq.co/?ref=rogerprice.me) is a good option.

For ***eCash*** (another direct method), [*Cashu*](https://cashu.me/?ref=rogerprice.me) is the way to go. Good alternatives are [*MiniBits*](https://github.com/minibits-cash/minibits_wallet?ref=rogerprice.me) and [*Macadamia*](https://macadamia.cash/?ref=rogerprice.me) (iOS). For larger communities of users, [*Fedi*](https://www.fedi.xyz/?ref=rogerprice.me) is a strong player - for example it is the The *go-to Wallet* on Bali and many other locations.

#### Indirectly

You have long been able to use Bitcoin to buy via [*Bitrefill*](https://www.bitrefill.com/gb/en/?ref=rogerprice.me) where you can buy Gift Cards, eSIMs and Mobile Top-up. More interesting is that there is now an increasing range of providers of prepaid *Visa* or *Mastercard* that you can top up with Bitcoin (and other cryptocurrencies).

A quick internet search will turn up plenty of good prepaid cards for your location - for example in Hong Kong we have:

- [**HashKey Exchange Card**](https://www.hashkey.com/en-US?ref=rogerprice.me) - Fast eKYC using the NFC reader on your smartphone for your HKID.
- [**RedotPay**](https://www.redotpay.com/?ref=rogerprice.me) **-** consistently highlighted for having the smoothest onboarding experience for locals since its app natively reads and processes HKID documents. They offer both physical and virtual cards, which are highly useful for tap-to-pay while traveling.
- [**Wirex**](https://www.wirexapp.com/uk-personal?ref=rogerprice.me) **-** A longstanding mainstream option for HK residents. Wirex is notable for offering competitive, near interbank real-time exchange rates. It provides a free monthly allowance for zero-fee international transactions.
- [**Halocard**](https://halocard.co/?ref=rogerprice.me) **-** A popular virtual card in Hong Kong because it is a US-issued Visa funded easily (and for free) via stablecoins.
- [**Kolo**](https://kolo.xyz/?ref=rogerprice.me) **-** Highlighted as a standout for zero foreign exchange (0% FX) fees and zero maintenance costs, while also paying out 2% in BTC rewards. It is ideal as a cost-effective secondary card exclusively for international trips. Kolo is also available in many other countries, including UK.

Treat these like your *Lightning* or *Cash wallet* - do not store more in them than you need for daily expenses - you can easily top them up when you need. Also, virtual cards are useful but pick options that also give you a physical card.

**One company to avoid is *Crypto.com***. They are the [*Ryanair*](https://rogerprice.me/165-weekend-in-antwerken/#:~:text=Time%20to%20boycott,companies%2C%20the%20better.) *of crypto*. ***Avoid them at all costs***. Their *support is dreadful* and I lost all faith in them due to their *mishandling of a fraudulent transaction* due to leak of card details from a restaurant website where you were required to enter details to guarantee the reservation. *We are older and wiser* - wisdom shared with you too!

### Dollar Cost Averaging (DCA)

Most people who are familiar with Bitcoin will recommend that you *consider it as a savings account*. You put some money into it in an ongoing basis - every day, week or month - whatever works best for you, regardless of the current price.

When you need, you can transfer bitcoin from your "*savings account*" to your spending wallet and use it. As the price of bitcoin goes up, you will benefit from increasing spending power - when the price goes down your DCA savings buy you more bitcon than before. Since you will have bought Bitcoin recently you become less sensitive to the price swings - in fact you can take advantage of them. The more who do this, the more valuable Bitcoin and your savings will become.

Likely some of you already know this and do it - do share your own experience and recommendations.
