### BIP-110 and all that

There has been a lot of drama in recent weeks around the BIP-110 soft fork and the potential chain split. This all came to a head last weekend and it is now over. TLDR: Nothing changes, there are lessons for all to learn and Bitcoin works as intended.

Two weeks ago I introduced you to [*Epistemological Warfare*](https://rogerprice.me/231-manchester-again/#:~:text=Thoughts-,Epistemological%20warfare,uncovering%20%2D%20more%20to%20follow.,-Epistemology) and how politicians (and hypnotists!) use Robert Dilts' "*Sleight of Mouth*" reframe constructions in their statements and arguments to bypass the normal defences in your brain and effectively "cast a spell on you" to make you believe them. Let's look at these techniques in action.

##### How it started

You should start here with the BIP-110 proposal that was put forward. Give it a read - at least skim through it.

> [!bookmark]
> [bips/bip-0110.mediawiki at master · bitcoin/bips](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0110.mediawiki?ref=rogerprice.me)
> Bitcoin Improvement Proposals. Contribute to bitcoin/bips development by creating an account on GitHub.
> — GitHub | ![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/9c/2a/9c2aa86d-156e-419f-9a0f-38bf91aebf15/content/images/thumbnail/bips-daf1b207-0457-4df5-969c-193057b95dde)

Click above to read - [here](https://rogerprice.me/bip-110-lets-analyse/) for my analysis

TLDR: By chunking up to “Bitcoin as money,” it makes data storage no longer an unapproved *use* but an *abuse* of infrastructure. By shrinking the temporal frame to one year, it neutralises the “precedent” and “slippery slope” objections before they can gain momentum. By redefining the fee market as two separate markets, it destroys the neutrality argument that fees alone should ration block space.

In Dilts’ terms, the text does not defend a rule change. It **redefines the rule change as boundary maintenance**.

##### The mainstream position

Then read Michael Saylor's 110 objections. It really is a masterpiece of writing and you will be wiser after you have read it and seen the techniques it contains.

> [!image]
> [![Click above to read -herefor my analysis](https://storage.ghost.io/c/9c/2a/9c2aa86d-156e-419f-9a0f-38bf91aebf15/content/images/2026/08/image-13.png)](https://www.strategy.com/media/articles/110-reasons-bip-110-is-a-bad-idea?ref=rogerprice.me)
> Click above to read -herefor my analysis

TLDR: Strategically, Saylor's article performs one overarching *Sleight of Mouth* manoeuvre:

> **It chunks the debate up from “How do we stop data storage?” to “Who gets to decide what Bitcoin is for?”**

By doing so, Saylor shifts the burden of proof entirely. The proponents must now defend not a technical limit, but a **governance philosophy**. He redefines BIP 110 from an engineering patch into a constitutional amendment that would break Bitcoin’s content neutrality.

I examined the articles above, testing for application of these techniques. Each article is packed full of them and (at least with hindsight) you ought to be able to see that this is intentional to set up two sides of an argument and to get people fighting with each other - rather than working through the issues seeking a "*third alternative*" - recall [our discussion](https://rogerprice.me/choices-you-de/#:~:text=The%207%20Habits,life%20and%20success) on that.

### Coldcard vulnerability - what to learn and do now

A five-year-old firmware bug silently replaced Coinkite's Coldcard hardware random number generator with a weaker one, cutting seed entropy from 128 bits to as few as 40. On July 30, 2026, an attacker brute-forced those weak seeds and drained over **2,000 BTC (~$130M)** from more than 7,700 wallets in a matter of minutes — without ever touching a single device.

The vulnerability was caused by a link-time symbol resolution error introduced during a codebase refactor, which "*inadvertently*" replaced the hardware True Random Number Generator with a weak software alternative. This flaw persisted for years because Coinkite's shift from open-source to a restrictive license discouraged the community auditing that could have detected such errors.

> [!bookmark]
> [Coldcard vulnerability](https://rogerprice.me/coldcard-vulnerability/)
> Origin of the Weakness The evidence points to a cascading series of events rooted in a license change, which created the conditions for a subtle but devastating build-integration bug. There is significant community debate about whether this was gross negligence, collateral damage from corporate decisions — or something more suspicious.
> — Letter from ...around the world | ![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/9c/2a/9c2aa86d-156e-419f-9a0f-38bf91aebf15/content/images/thumbnail/Coldcard-9bf1db48-6f85-4022-99ac-dc059fdeb31a.jpeg)

Remember: There are no coincidences. Look at the evidence.

Knowing all this, what can you do going forward? Firstly, avoid Coldcard and move any funds away immediately - Coinkite cannot be trusted. Going forward you need to favour *open source* and well-audited offerings. Be very suspicious of other closed source or restrictive-license products. Other best practices include the use of BIP-39 passphrases - these are easy to use immediately. Longer term *Multi-Sig* solutions should become easier to use and may have wider adoption.

*CakeWallet* is well known for its *Monero* support. In recent years they also have excellent Bitcoin support, including for lightning transfers. They also offer interesting features like [CupCake](https://rogerprice.me/cupcake/). Notwithstanding the name - this is a seriously interesting open source and well-validated option and a good use of an otherwise obsolete phone - it can certainly form part of your setup - along with Monero.
