# 🛠️ Project Updates

This week I am sharing some hands-on updates from what I have been doing with *open-weight LLMs* and using them to dig deeper into all sorts of knowledge sources. Also, I included some practical guidance for those wanting to join in. What are you doing?

### Obsidian - it keeps getting better

Recently I have been doing more with Obsidian. This is a fantastic note-taking software that runs on Mac, Linux, iOS and Android. While the app is not open source, your data is stored entirely in markdown (.md) text files so you have no proprietary lock-in and you can process your data freely with all tools at your disposal. The company also actively encourages the community to develop extensions and plug-ins that are freely available and add all sorts of useful features.

##### Letter from around the world

First project was to make an Obsidian vault containing *all of my newsletters.* This serves as a backup (I now have it under git too) and it is fully searchable using all Linux tooling. Of course I use *Syncthing* to have it available on other machines where I need it and to ensure offsite backup. I also have a one-line script to run each week to update the vault with the latest newsletter.

I went further and installed *Ollama*. This is an open-source software platform and runtime designed for downloading, running and managing large language models (LLMs) locally on your own hardware. Ollama is *not an AI model itself,* but rather the management layer and runtime that allows you to execute various *open-weigh*t models .

I have been experimenting with some of the *open-weigh*t models that you can run locally to index and query my newsletters. Fascinating results so far. Given that the newsletters are public, a next stage will be to experiment with some of the larger models using *nano-gpt.*

##### Chronicon

I now have a year-by-year history of the world with all of Jason source notes and his dating calculations fully available locally. This too is now also searchable with my local AI that I am running.

A next step for this will likely be to also incorporate James Ussher's "[*The Annals of the World*](https://archive.org/details/james-usher-annals-of-the-world)" into the vault and then to get AI to look for interesting correlations, corroborations and contradictions.

### Exiting Big Tech more generally

We have discussed many times about how you really should reduce and eliminate Big Tech subscriptions. The best way to do this is to self-host services that can replace the subscription services that you use. Big Tech subscriptions are designed to lock you in to their walled gardens and they will continue to suck out ever increasing subscription fees while making it difficult to leave.

SAC reflects on over a year of running her own servers and she does have some rather good tips to keep in mind as you plan out what you will host yourself.

> [!image]
> [![Good reflections on a year of self-hosting - do listen and learn](https://storage.ghost.io/c/9c/2a/9c2aa86d-156e-419f-9a0f-38bf91aebf15/content/images/2026/07/image-39.png)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FqfFuruBLkI&ref=rogerprice.me)
> Good reflections on a year of self-hosting - do listen and learn

- host things you use daily - this will give immediate and ongoing value
- write down config and passwords
- have backups that work (tested with documentation)

It's true, it's too easy to get sucked into trying all sorts of things at once rather than focusing on getting your first service working properly with good backup and then expanding. Also, if you do not write down all the configuration details you will find yourself stuck in a few months when you want to change or update it!

Remember the *3-2-1 backup rule*. Have at least *three backups* using *two different media* with at least *one of them stored offsite*. All of them should be tested - otherwise you can consider them as null and void!
