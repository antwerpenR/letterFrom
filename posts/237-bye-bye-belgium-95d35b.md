🇧🇪 🇵🇹 As this newsletter goes out, V and I are back on the road to our next destination. We last visited it for our honeymoon, all those years ago so we are looking forward to getting back to visit again. Our stay this time will be a bit shorter than planned due to our extended trip in Belgium, but that is the joy of travelling!

Due to the last minute nature of the Belgian stay I forgot that I had declined to go to the *Swift Alumni gathering* in *La Hulpe* since I was expecting to be abroad! Apart from that V & I have done about everything that we had planned for this trip. We are heading *South* for a few days of *R&R* and then one last swing *North* for family and friends before we head East for the winter.

### Fedimint - it is getting real

Thanks to Matt Odell for yet another fantastically informative interview. Thanks also to Eric, Joscha and Hermann for all the work they have been doing. Fedi is an important component in enabling Bitcoin to be used as money in and between communities.

Like *Cashu* it is based on *Chaumian eCash*, however unlike Cashu it aims for out-of-the-box deployment into communities where it enables and empowers local community leaders to take ownership for running the infrastructure.

Regarding Omarchy, it certainly seems to be remarkable in many respects and I loved the discussion on how Linux can, and will, likely become dominant for end-users in the coming years. # 🤔 Closing Thoughts

Some four years ago I recommended *Matthew Walker's book*, "[*Why we sleep*](https://rogerprice.me/25-a-week-in-antwerp/#:~:text=%F0%9F%93%9A,must%21)" as book of the week. Check it out and refresh your memory [here](https://rogerprice.me/25-a-week-in-antwerp/#:~:text=%F0%9F%93%9A,must%21). With four years of additional insights and some hindsight this may well have been "*Selective Disclosure" - ie intended to give people so much knowledge and insight (mixed with a bit of misleading disinformation) than they would be satisfied and not want to investigate further.*

For those who also see this, a first book to pick up is *Robert Monroe's book, "Journeys out of the body"*, written way back in 1971. I have been reading it recently and it is a fascinating *predecessor companion* to Matt's book.

In the book, Monroe recounts how, unpredictably and without willing it, he found himself leaving his physical body to travel in a "*second body*" to locales far removed from physical and spiritual reality, unbound by time or death. He describes the disconcerting "vibrations" that precede separation, and organises his journeys by theme into three realms, alongside practical tips on how to get out of your body, all told with wry humour.

I am well aware that much of this discussion is controversial and that, even among those those who have heard of *The Monroe Institute,* there is a lot of skepticism about the origin and motivation of much of what he describes, not least in the (im)famous 30 page CIA document.
