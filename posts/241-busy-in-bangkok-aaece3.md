🇹🇭 We are really happy to be back in South East Asia again - it does feel like coming home. We are not yet back in Hong Kong - we had lots to do here in Bangkok first. We have been fully busy every day, starting with our morning swim in the pool on the roof.

First priority here was to find an apartment so we got straight to it. We have several favourite buildings that we know well and are rather convenient - also quite a few landlords/owners that we know now from previous visits, just like in all the locations that we visit. That done, next step was to get everything in the apartment the way that V likes it. Needless to say, this entailed a few visits to Ikea.

We also took advantage of the excellent medical and dental facilities. It is beyond me how people tolerate (and even extol) the services that you find in the UK and some other locations. From our experience you will find decent services in some European countries but what you find here in Bangkok is world class and best value. For pretty much anything, you can simply phone and they ask you if you want to come this afternoon or tomorrow.

Notwithstanding the fearmongering that you see on the news (Empire propaganda channels), the weather here has been OK. It is rainy season and there is certainly flooding in some parts of Bangkok and around but in Central Bangkok everything is fully normal. We also had the advantage of flying Qatar airways which has separate luggage handling, so arrival in Bangkok was also fully smooth - those flying Thai airways were not so lucky.

#### Viktor Frankl: *Man's Search for Meaning*

*Viktor Frankl* was a Viennese psychiatrist who survived Auschwitz and three other Nazi camps, where his parents, brother and pregnant wife perished. Written in nine days and first published in German in 1946, his book has sold more than 16 million copies in 52 languages and remains one of the most influential works of the twentieth century.

> [!bookmark]
> [Man’s Search for Meaning](https://a.co/d/068cj5Gw?ref=rogerprice.me)
> Man’s Search for Meaning [Frankl, Viktor E., Winslade, William J., Kushner, Harold S.] on Amazon.com. \*FREE\* shipping on qualifying offers. Man’s Search for Meaning

Click for the book - summary below

The book has two halves. The first is testimony: Frankl's account of camp life, traced through three psychological phases — the shock of arrival, the long apathy of imprisonment, and the disorientation of liberation. He observed that prisoners who clung to a task, a person or a future meaning tended to endure, while those who lost their "why" often died within days. His guiding line, borrowed from Nietzsche: *"He who has a why to live can bear almost any how."*

The second half expounds **logotherapy**, his "therapy through meaning." Frankl argues that the primary human drive is not pleasure (Freud) or power (Adler) but **the will to meaning**. When that will is frustrated, we fall into an **existential vacuum** — the modern malaise of boredom and emptiness, which he called noogenic neurosis. Meaning, he insists, cannot be invented but must be detected, and it is found in three ways:

- **Through work** — a task or creative achievement to fulfil.
- **Through love** — an encounter with another person.
- **Through suffering** — the attitude one adopts toward unavoidable pain, which can be transformed into an achievement.

The book's enduring claim is stark and consoling: circumstances may bring enormous challenges, but the last of human freedoms is the freedom to choose our attitude in any given set of circumstances. Recall that this echoes exactly the sentiment and wisdom that I have long extolled in [*The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People*](https://rogerprice.me/choices-you-de/#:~:text=The%207%20Habits,life%20and%20success).
