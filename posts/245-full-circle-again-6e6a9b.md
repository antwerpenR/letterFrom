# 🤔 Closing Thoughts

I am closing out this week with a book recommendation. Normally these are up front as "*Book of the Week*" but I feel this one may be a step further than many will feel comfortable with. Nonetheless, I encourage you to take it. I have had the book for over a year but finally got pushed to read it this week by a local subscriber - Thanks!

#### Visionary

Visionary is a book by Graham Hancock that explores the mystery of how early humans suddenly developed complex creativity, art and religion. He frames this as a detective story, using a method that combines personal experience with research. He highlights the role of shamanism (a practice involving a practitioner who enters a trance to communicate with the spirit world) and psychedelics (substances that alter perception and consciousness) in human evolution.

> [!bookmark]
> [Visionary](https://rogerprice.me/visionary/)
> Visionary is Graham Hancock's 2022 book — but it isn't a new work so much as the "definitive edition" of his 2005 book Supernatural: Meetings with the Ancient Teachers of Mankind. It restores the original unabridged text, adds a new introduction, and reinstates chapters cut from the earlier paperback
> — Letter from ...around the world | ![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/9c/2a/9c2aa86d-156e-419f-9a0f-38bf91aebf15/content/images/thumbnail/Screenshot-2026-11-03-at-10.20.11-1a7e0937-cfc2-4a65-8e46-7fe29dfddf44.png)

Click for summary and chapter by chapter notes

Relying on the *neuropsychological model* (a scientific framework) he suggests that cave art reflects visions experienced during deep trance states to explain why ancient cave paintings frequently feature therianthropes (mythical beings that are part-human and part-animal).

He concludes by challenging standard evolutionary biology, proposing that human consciousness may have been intentionally guided or influenced by these visionary experiences, rather than emerging solely through random genetic mutations.

I have long been critical of Graham regarding his position on some other topics but this seems to be a solid, academically rigorous and personal exploration account. In it, he covers many of the topics that I think are really important and you will learn from his experience and insight here.
