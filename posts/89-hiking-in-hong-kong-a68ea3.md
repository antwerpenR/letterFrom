🇭🇰 This week we have been doing the maximum to get out and about and enjoy Hong Kong at its best. Saturday I was out with *Eye Opener Hiking Group* on a great hike from Mei Foo, Sunday I went with a few friends from DB to Mui Wo. On Monday V and I went up to the Peak and came back down on the newly renovated (and now rather expensive) Peak Tram. Tuesday was another Peak Hike up the Escalator from bottom to top. Wednesday was a group hike Wong Nai Chung Gap over Violet Hill and down to Repulse Bay. Thursday and Friday I got in another Peak Hike and V and I went to enjoy lunch at the Windsurfing club on Cheung Chau.

HK really should be like this all year round!

I am also busy with a bunch of business things that need to get done when we are here in HK. It is true that HK business administration is lighter than many places but the monster is here too, albeit somewhat below the surface. On the positive side it is still relatively easy here to pay for others to deal with the machine for you and they are well motivated to give you a good service.

I have also been meeting up with local bitcoiners here in HK. These really are the most interesting people to meet when you travel. One of my subscribers from HK is even nipping over to Bangkok for a few days for Bitcoin conferences and events that I am missing by being here in HK! So much going on.

Saturday we are doing a great hike to *Shing Mun Redoubt* - video from the last time we were there is below - should be good! If you are in HK - you can still sign up and join us [here](https://www.meetup.com/eye-opener-hk-hiking-%E9%96%8B%E7%9C%BC%E7%95%8C%E8%A1%8C%E5%B1%B1%E9%9A%8A/events/296554596/?utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=share-btn_savedevents_share_modal&utm_source=link).

### Book of the week

This week's book is 60 pages; I first read it quite some years ago but was reminded of it by a recent discussion with a subscriber (thanks!) - I even found my marked-up copy. It is a short read and the [audiobook](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qrOPBXrLWoA&ref=rogerprice.me) is just 60 minutes. Read it yourself and you will see the world differently. So many things that may have confused you will become absolutely clear.

> [!bookmark]
> [Anatomy of the State | Murray N. Rothbard](https://mises.org/library/anatomy-state?ref=rogerprice.me)
> This gives a succinct account of Rothbard’s view of the state. Following Franz Oppenheimer and Albert Jay Nock, Rothbard regards the state as a predatory entity. It does not produce anything but rather steals resources from those engaged in production.
> — Mises Institute | ![](https://cdn.mises.org/styles/social\_media\_1200\_x\_1200/s3/social-media/anatomy-of-the-state\_rothbard\_1200x630\_20210419.jpg?itok=zX1izQM8)

Click for the book - free in multiple formats

Here are just a few of the snippets of wisdom you will see substantiated and explained with examples that may help you spot them:

- The State is that organisation in society which attempts to maintain a monopoly of the use of force and violence in a given territorial area; in particular, it is the only organisation in society that obtains its revenue not by voluntary contribution or payment for services rendered but *by coercion*.
- The State provides a legal, orderly, systematic channel for the predation of private property; it renders certain, secure, and relatively “peaceful” the lifeline of the *parasitic caste* in society. Since production must always precede predation, the free market is anterior to the State. The State has never been created by a “social contract”; it has always been born in conquest and exploitation.
- Once a State has been established, the problem of the ruling group or “caste” is how to maintain their rule. While force is their modus operandi, their basic and long- run problem is ideological. For in order to continue in office, any government (not simply a “democratic” government) must have the support of the majority of its subjects. This support, it must be noted, need not be active enthusiasm; it may well be *passive resignation* as if to an inevitable law of nature.
- Therefore, the chief task of the rulers is always to secure the active or resigned acceptance of the majority of the citizens.
- It is important for the State to make its rule seem inevitable; even if its reign is disliked, it will then be met with passive resignation, as witness the familiar coupling of “death and taxes.”

#### Network State

You will recall that I have long maintained that we are living through Chapter 10 of [The Sovereign Individual](https://rogerprice.me/holywood-and-belfast-just-visiting/#%F0%9F%93%9A-book-of-the-week) - *The Twilight of Democracy* . As with the earlier chapters of the book that so accurately predicted just about everything significant that has happened so far this century (including the *plandemic*), so too Chapter 10 seems to be spot on.

One of the things that the authors expected was the emergence of *jurisdictional arbitrage* where islands, special administrative regions and similar constructions would emerge - enabling people to *opt ou*t of the corruption and collapse into something better. It is interesting to consider that Hong Kong was the star example of such SARs and it certainly has been till recent years; to be seen if that is a title that it can reclaim or not.

Recall that Balaji has been expounding this theory too and he, along with many others are now experimenting and taking practical steps to make things happen. The material from the recent conference in Amsterdam is below - watch for your edification - do not say that nobody told you.
